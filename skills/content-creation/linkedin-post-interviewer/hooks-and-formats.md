# LinkedIn hooks, formats, voice, and rules (synthesised from linkedin-writer, post-writer, sergebulaev/linkedin-skills, AItomation skill)

## Hook patterns
From post-writer (11): Reframe, Parallel Contrast, Specific Number, Question, Vulnerable Story, Contrarian Take,
Pain Point List, Behind-the-Scenes, Receipts ("I did X for N days. Here's what happened."), "Most People"
Reverse, Stolen Lesson ("I copied X. Here's what happened.").
Named formulas from sergebulaev's Post Writer (it lists 10; only 4 names were visible to me): anaphora (repeat
an opening phrase), R.I.P. obituary ("R.I.P. [old way]"), year-over-year pivot, curiosity gap. Do not invent the
other six; use the 11 above.
AItomation rule: never open with a "Ever wonder..." question. Lead with a claim, a number, or a scene.
Best for build-in-public: Receipts, Specific Number, Vulnerable Story, Behind-the-Scenes.

## 7 formats (linkedin-writer)
Lessons Learned | Blueprint (step-by-step) | Story | Strategy | Case Study | Hot Take | Quick Hack.
For day-N updates: Story, Lessons Learned, or Case Study (with the honest gap).

## Structure (AItomation + post-writer)
1 Hook: works at feed truncation, about the first 140 characters / 2 lines before "see more".
2 Body: 3-6 short paragraphs, line breaks every 1-2 sentences, in the order you would say it aloud.
3 Turn: one line that reframes or contradicts the obvious take. The line people screenshot.
4 Close: a takeaway or ONE genuine, specific question. Never "Thoughts?" or "Agree?".
ONE claim per post. If there are two ideas, save one for another post.

## Length and format
- Target 900-1,300 characters (sergebulaev), up to about 1,500 for a story (post-writer). Stories up to 400 words
  only if every line earns its place.
- Links go in the post body (founder decision 31 Oct overrides the old first-comment advice from post-writer):
  after the story, before the closing ask, never in the hook lines.
- Hashtags: 0-3 (theme.md cap), at the end, chosen per the Hashtag strategy below. Never #motivation
  #growth #leadership.
- No em dashes. No emojis unless the user's voice uses them.
- Banned: leverage, unlock, delve, harness, streamline, foster, fundamentally, game-changer, disrupt,
  revolutionize, synergy, "in today's fast-paced world". Rule-of-three lists are an AI tell: vary list lengths.
- Capitalise names and products. Digits for numbers. Specific numbers beat adjectives.
- Contractions yes. Active voice. Short sentences; break anything over 20 words.

## Hashtag strategy (maximize views)
Hashtags are a discovery lever on LinkedIn: posts get indexed in each tag's feed, and the tags help the
algorithm classify the post to the right audience. Output a hashtag set with every draft: 0-3 tags (theme.md
cap), CamelCase, 1-3 words each, placed at the end of the post, never mid-sentence.

Tier mix (this is what maximizes views; within the 0-3 cap prioritize 1 mid-size niche tag + 1
community/branded tag, the third slot is optional):
- 1 broad category tag (high volume, e.g. #AI, #EdTech, #FutureOfLearning): you will not rank here, but it
  sharpens how the classifier distributes the post.
- 2-3 mid-size niche tags (roughly 5k-200k followers, e.g. #BuildInPublic, #VoiceAI, #STEMEducation,
  #PhysicsLearning): where a small account can actually appear in the tag feed and hold attention. This tier
  does most of the reach work.
- 1 community/branded tag (low competition, e.g. #GnaniAI, #GreatIndianAIInternshipChallenge): you can rank
  near the top of its recent feed, and for this project the organisers and judges follow these tags.

Before using a tag, open its page and check follower count and posts added in the last day. A tag with 1M+
followers and thousands of daily posts buries you instantly; swap in a niche variant. Never use generic
motivation tags (#motivation #growth #leadership #success) or tags unrelated to the post: hashtag spam dilutes
classification and draws bot engagement. Never exceed 3 (theme.md cap).
Present the set as: tag, tier, one-line reason, plus 1-2 alternates. Treat reach effects as hypotheses; never
promise views.

## Reach claims (treat as hypotheses)
Third-party sources say comments carry about 2x the weight of likes and that the first hours matter. These come
from marketing sites, not LinkedIn documentation. Use them to justify a comment-reply plan, never to promise reach.

## Formats of media
Single image, short native video with burned-in captions, or a document/carousel. Ask which the user can produce
today. Vertical 4:5 or square is a reasonable default for phone-heavy feeds; confirm against the current composer.

## Voice
Derive from 3-5 of the user's real posts (AItomation method): sentence length, openers, closers, repeated words,
things they never do. If none exist, ask for 3 messages or notes they wrote. Never describe voice with adjectives
like "authentic" or "engaging".

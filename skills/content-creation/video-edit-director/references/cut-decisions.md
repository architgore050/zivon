# Cut decisions (synthesised from video-editing-pro and the storyboard/retention skills)

## Sentence scoring (for any speech)
Score each sentence 0-3: new information, energy, clarity, memorability. When length forces a choice, cut the lowest
totals (about 4 or less). Read the whole transcript before cutting anything.

Per-sentence flow:
- Strongest opener (action verb, bold claim, contrast)? Hook candidate.
- Advances the story, proves a claim, or delivers payoff? Keep. Transition or restatement? Cut.
- List with 3+ items? Keep the 2 strongest, or reframe as a number ("I built 3 systems in hours") and drop the list.
- After the cut, do the lines before and after still connect? If not, keep a bridge or reorder.

## Pauses
- Under about 100 ms: leave alone (cutting clips consonants).
- 150-300 ms between sentences: cut to about 150-200 ms. 300-500 ms: cut in fast beats; keep as breathing room in
  emotional beats. Over 500 ms: cut.
- Keep the breath after a strong claim; it carries belief.
- Add about 200 ms of room tone at joins so cuts do not sound robotic.
- Cut on the leading consonant of the next word, not the trailing vowel.
(Numbers are practitioner heuristics from the sources; adjust by ear.)

## Fillers
Always cut: mid-sentence um/uh, stutters (keep the clean take), "you know"/"I mean" openers. Leave natural texture
that makes the speaker sound human. Over-cleaned speech sounds assembled. A few raw edges are a trust signal for a
personal brand.

## Hook selection
- The hook is the sharpest action-or-contrast moment in the whole footage, not the first sentence. Scan everything.
- Lands in 1.5-3 s. Banned openers: "Hey guys", "Welcome back", "So today I want to talk about".
- Open loop: question or claim now, answer later ("Watch this." beats a 4 s explanation).
- Test: mute it. Do the first 3 s still communicate the promise through text and picture?
- Silent-test fail = rewrite the opener, not the body.

Hook types that suit build-in-public: Number ("0 domains, 1 tester, day 1"), Receipts ("I did X for N days"),
Visual interrupt (start mid-action on the result), Unfinished story ("I almost skipped X until..."),
Contrarian (only if backed by what you actually did).

## Energy curve
- 0-3 s HIGH (fast, claim, loop). 3-15 s medium-high (one new fact every 3-5 s). Approaching the peak, cuts get
  faster (about 4 s, then 3 s, then 2 s). Just before the peak, one 300-500 ms beat of relative quiet. Peak: the
  demo reveal or strongest proof. Close: medium, one clear takeaway or ask.
- Uniform pacing = no peak = no rewatch.
- Visual change at least every 5-8 s. Sources of change: cut, punch-in, new text card, caption emphasis, switch to
  screen recording.
- L-cuts: let the voice start about 0.5 s before cutting to the screen recording; cut mid-sentence, never on the
  last word.

## Length
Aim for the shortest version that still delivers hook, proof, peak, close. 20-35 s is a common sweet spot for
feeds; 45 s is a practical ceiling for most build-in-public clips. These are heuristics, not rules.

## Common v1 mistakes (from a real clip post-mortem)
Cutting the one genuinely new proof sentence while keeping list restatements; ending the vision line mid-sentence;
leaving a 900 ms dead pause before the CTA; switching language mid-video with no bridge; keeping announcement
filler ("coming soon") with no payoff.

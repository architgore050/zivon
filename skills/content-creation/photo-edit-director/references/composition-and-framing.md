# Composition and framing for feed images

## What to check (from photo-edit critique practice)
- Framing and subject placement: rule of thirds, leading lines, balance, negative space.
- Depth: foreground, mid-ground, background. Does the image have layers?
- Point of entry: where does the eye land first, and where does it travel? That path should end on the proof.
- Horizon and verticals: level unless tilt is intentional.
- Crop: is anything cut off that should not be, or included that should not be?
- Light as a compositional element: does it help or fight the subject?

## Feed rules
- Thumbnail test: shrink to about 300 px wide. If the subject or the key text is unreadable, crop tighter or
  enlarge the text. Most feed viewing is a thumb-sized phone image.
- One subject. If you need two, make them clearly primary and secondary.
- Tight beats wide for proof: cut the browser chrome, desktop wallpaper, menu bars, and any unrelated UI.
- Leave breathing room (padding) around cropped screenshots; a flat or soft-gradient background looks deliberate.
- Keep faces, logos, and critical text away from platform-cropped edges.

## Format starting points (confirm in the current composer)
- 4:5 portrait or 1:1 square: strong default for phone feeds. 16:9: good for link previews and desktop.
- 9:16 for full-screen vertical (stories/reels covers). Keep key content in the central safe area.
- Export at least 1080 px on the short side; keep text large enough that no letter is under about 4 percent of the
  image height.

## Photos of people or places (real photos)
- Eye level, window light in front of you or the subject, no overhead lights, no backlit windows behind faces.
- Real desk, real whiteboard, real notebook beat stock-looking set-ups.
- Clean the background: remove clutter by moving objects before the shot (not by editing them out afterwards).

A — The 8pm Doubt

File: concept-a.html · page proposal: / · entrant: referral · awareness: solution-aware · emotion: relief

Brief

Who lands here
A Class 9 student, on a phone, at 8pm, from a classmate’s link: “this one explains it properly”.
What they believe
“There are a hundred apps that claim to teach me Physics. Most are videos, or a chatbot with a textbook stapled on. I’ll give this thirty seconds.”
One action
Open a chapter and start reading; ask the first doubt if they want to.
What must be true
The first screen must show the page answering out loud, anchored to the paragraph, and free without an account.
Honest emotion
Relief — “oh, that’s all it was”.
Must not exploit
Fear of boards; parental disappointment; shame; fake urgency; a fake student testimonial.
Promise
Ask the page. Out loud. In Hinglish.
Substitution test
A competitor cannot say it without building page-anchored voice. Pass (N — it becomes false).
CTA
Open a chapter · same words in header, hero and final. After click: the live chapter list, first live chapter first. Friction removed: no account.
Proof available
Six live lesson pages, a real source footer, a check/feedback pattern, and a sample transcript. No real student quote yet.

Narrative spine

Default cold/referral spine: Hook → Tension → Guide → Plan → Proof → Offer → Action. The reader already believes the category; the job is to show the mechanism and remove the risk. I rejected PAS because it would agitate a child; AIDA because it is a diagnostic, not a structure; BAB because the transformation is too large to show in one screen; StoryBrand as a separate spine because the guide/plan relationship already sits inside the default; FAB because it is a one-section lens, not a page.

Why this should work — rubric case (not a self-score)

D1 hook: the mechanism, language and transcript are in the first screen; a five-second reader can say what it is and who it is for. D3 desire: relief is the emotion the reader arrives with; the page resolves one doubt instead of threatening an exam. D5 differentiation: page-anchored voice + Hinglish cannot be copied without building it. D4 proof: the transcript, diagram, source ledger and check are specific artifacts; the sample is labelled. D7 accessibility: transcript visible without JS or audio; no autoplay; 360 px first. D9 honesty: no testimonial, no “official”, coverage names what is not ready. Anticipated weakness: the proof is a sample until a real consented recording exists; B’s coverage is broader if the reader is exam-focused.

What would make it fail: a synthetic transcript presented as a real student; a language claim without a native Hinglish read; a hero that hides the CTA below the 360×640 fold; a second CTA competing with “Open a chapter”.

Measurement plan

Primary: chapter_open ÷ landing_view (unique session) over 28 days. Diagnostics: cta_try placement, scroll to coverage, FAQ open; no demo event because there is no audio player in this concept. Qualitative: 5-second test n=5, sceptic read n=2. Decision rule: if chapter_open rises ≥15% relative with no drop in 5-second recall, ship the variant; otherwise iterate the hero. Consent-gated events; no child free text; no ad pixels.

B — The Marked Paper

File: concept-b.html · page proposal: /board-practice (or / if chosen) · entrant: search/exam-stakes · awareness: problem-aware → product-aware · emotion: momentum

Brief

Who lands here
A Class 10 student on a phone, searching for a chapter test or following a link from a class group, two weeks before a school test.
What they believe
“I need practice papers, not another video. I don’t know where I’m actually losing marks.”
One action
Open a Class 10 chapter test and attempt it; no account for the first test.
What must be true
The first screen must show a result that turns a score into a gap and a next step — not another promise of “practice”.
Honest emotion
Momentum — “I know what to fix next.”
Must not exploit
Fear of boards; rank/leaderboard; parental disappointment; fake “official CBSE”; fake scarcity.
Promise
Know what you actually know.
Substitution test
A generic test-prep product can say “practice test”, but not “marks the gap, then gives you the next concept” without the review map. Pass (N).
CTA
Open a test · header, hero, final. After click: the first live Class 10 chapter test. Friction removed: no account to try; no negative marking.
Proof available
A sample marked paper (replace with a live capture), a review artifact, the no-rank policy, the non-affiliation copy; Class 9–10 chapter tests are a [launch] assumption.

Narrative spine

Mechanism-first: Hook → Tension → Mechanism → Proof → Offer → Objection → Action. The reader is product-aware enough to compare test tools; the differentiator is the gap map, not the existence of a test. Rejected: default hook-first because the category is known; PAS because it would use fear; BAB because this is not a launch; StoryBrand as the spine because the guide/plan is already inside the mechanism; AIDA as a checklist.

Cross-concept decisions

These are the same decisions listed on the review index; the answer determines which page becomes the product landing page and which become campaign routes.

  1. Which reader owns /? Recommendation: A for /; B for a board-practice campaign URL; C for /about or a launch log. The alternative is C for / if the founder wants the build-in-public story to be the front door.
  2. A: mechanism or tension headline? Chosen mechanism. The tension variant is a fair test but risks a negative first screen for a child.
  3. B: outcome or objection headline? Chosen outcome. The objection variant is stronger for product-aware traffic; it can be the campaign hero.
  4. Coverage posture. B assumes launch-state Class 9–10; A uses the current Class 9 state; C uses the current six-page state. Decide whether the landing promises the launch catalogue or ships the current catalogue and grows. This is a claim-ledger decision, not a copy preference.
  5. Artifacts. A’s transcript and B’s result are labelled samples. Accept for the direction verdict, or require a real consented recording and a live capture before implementation?
  6. CTA destination. The concepts anchor to their proof sections. In production: catalogue or strongest live chapter? Recommendation: strongest live chapter, no account gate.
  7. The “I” voice. C uses the founder’s voice. If C is not /about, should a public hook page use “I”, or should it stay neutral?

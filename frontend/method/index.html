Method

A page, a tutor, and a check.

Zivon is built around one loop: read the page, see the idea move, answer a question, and ask your own version of the doubt. This page explains how that loop is made and where it stops.

Start from the source.

Every lesson is written from an NCERT chapter or an openly licensed source, and the page names the book and pages it came from. Sources are not decoration: the fact review compares the lesson against that pack, block by block.

Lessons are paraphrased, not reproduced. Zivon is not affiliated with NCERT or CBSE, and a Zivon page is not an official textbook page or an official exam paper.

Explain the step, not just the answer.

The tutor is patient and step-by-step. It points at the paragraph or diagram the student is looking at, explains the reasoning, and then asks a question back. It is not designed to hand over a final answer to copy.

It can still be wrong. That is why the source line sits on the lesson page and the problem report is available beside the content. A confident sentence is not proof; the source is.

Make the idea move.

Diagrams are drawn as clean geometry. Simulations compute their numbers from the equations in the lesson and show their readouts as text, so the page still makes sense to a screen reader or on a slow phone. Every interactive page has a performance budget on a low-end Android profile; a pretty simulation that janks is a defect.

Ask the student to retrieve.

Short checks sit after paragraphs. The feedback names the reasoning and explains the common slip, instead of showing only a tick. A check is not a score badge; it tells the student which idea to look at again.

Five-question practice, chapter tests and test series are not built yet. The catalogue marks what a chapter has instead of offering a link that cannot open.

Voice, in the language the student thinks in.

Speech is transcribed, answered, and spoken back in English, Hindi or Hinglish. Raw audio is not kept. The transcript has a 7-day server retention window and can be deleted from the privacy page. Reading, simulations and checks do not depend on voice or a microphone.

Live lessons are fully translated. A missing translation is a publish failure, not a silent English fallback.

How a page is made.

  1. Curate. Pick the NCERT chapter and build its source pack.
  2. Generate. Turn the chapter into structured text, diagram, simulation and check blocks.
  3. Build the visuals. Render the diagrams and simulations as self-contained HTML, SVG and canvas.
  4. Check the physics. Recompute the answers and compare the lesson with the source pack.
  5. Review the code and the page. No external calls, no broken accessibility, no performance regressions.
  6. Translate and publish. English, Hindi and Hinglish, then the published catalogue.

This is the pipeline that runs today. It is not a claim that every page is perfect; it is the reason a page is allowed to be public.

What we do not claim.

  • Zivon is not affiliated with CBSE or NCERT, and no page is an official board paper.
  • Zivon does not replace a teacher, a textbook, or solving problems with a pencil.
  • We do not promise a marks increase, a rank, or a guaranteed exam result.
  • Accounts, practice, chapter tests and test series are not built yet. The status page says so plainly.

Motion in a Plane

After this lesson you can:

  • After this lesson you can tell whether a quantity is a scalar or a vector and add vectors graphically and by components.
  • After this lesson you can find position, velocity and acceleration in a plane from components and from differentiation.
  • After this lesson you can solve constant-acceleration, projectile and uniform circular motion problems using the equations of this chapter.

You throw a ball and it moves sideways and up at the same time. A plus or minus sign cannot describe that sideways-plus-up direction. You need vectors, quantities that carry both magnitude and direction.

In this lesson you will learn the language of vectors, then use it for position, velocity and acceleration in a plane. You will then solve constant-acceleration motion, projectile motion and uniform circular motion.

A scalar has magnitude only and is given by a number with a unit. Distance, mass, temperature and time are scalars. A vector has magnitude and direction and obeys triangle or parallelogram addition. Displacement, velocity, acceleration and force are vectors.

Choose origin O. The position vector OP equals r points from O to P. If the object moves from P to P prime, displacement PP prime is the straight line joining start and finish. It does not depend on the actual path, and its magnitude is less than or equal to path length.

Which list contains only vector quantities?

Why?

Displacement, velocity and acceleration have magnitude and direction and obey vector addition. The other lists are scalars.

Multiplying A by positive lambda gives magnitude lambda times |A| in the same direction. Multiplying by -lambda reverses direction. Multiplying a velocity by a time gives a displacement.

Two vectors are equal only if magnitude and direction match. Shifting parallel to itself leaves a free vector unchanged. A null vector has zero magnitude and no direction, and A plus 0 equals A.

Head-to-tail method: place tail of B at head of A, join tail of A to head of B to get R. Parallelogram method: bring tails to common origin and complete the parallelogram; R is the diagonal. Both give the same R.

Addition is commutative and associative. Subtraction is A minus B equals A plus (-B). Adding A and -A gives the null vector.

Worked example

Rain is falling vertically with a speed of 35 m s-1. Wind starts blowing with a speed of 12 m s-1 in east to west direction. In which direction should a boy hold his umbrella?

  1. Represent velocities Let vr be rain velocity vertically down and vw be wind velocity east to west. Resultant R is their vector sum.
  2. Magnitude R = sqrt(vr squared + vw squared) = sqrt(35 squared + 12 squared) = 37 m s-1.
  3. Direction tan theta = vw / vr = 12 / 35 = 0.343, so theta = tan inverse 0.343 = 19 degrees from the vertical.

Answer Hold umbrella in the vertical plane at about 19 degrees with the vertical towards the east, as given in the source.

Which algebraic operation with scalar and vector quantities is meaningful in general?

Why?

A vector multiplied by a scalar is a vector with scaled magnitude. Adding scalar to vector, or scalars of different units, is not meaningful.

A vector A in a plane can be resolved along two non-parallel directions. Using rectangular axes, unit vectors i, j, k have magnitude 1 and point along x, y, z axes.

Write A equals Ax i plus Ay j. Ax and Ay are scalars; Ax i and Ay j are vectors. In three dimensions add Az k. Position vector is r equals x i plus y j plus z k.

From magnitude-angle to components and back

Given A and theta

Ax = A cos theta and Ay = A sin theta from trigonometry on the component diagram.

Given Ax and Ay

A = sqrt(Ax squared + Ay squared) by Pythagoras, and tan theta = Ay / Ax.

Signs

A component can be positive, negative or zero depending on theta, because cos and sin change sign by quadrant.

Analytical addition: write A equals Ax i plus Ay j and B equals Bx i plus By j. Then R equals (Ax plus Bx) i plus (Ay plus By) j. Each resultant component is the sum of corresponding components.

In three dimensions add z components the same way. This extends to any number of vectors, with subtraction as addition of the negative.

Worked example

Find the magnitude and direction of the resultant of two vectors A and B in terms of their magnitudes and angle theta between them.

  1. Parallelogram geometry Let OS be resultant R. ON = A + B cos theta and SN = B sin theta, with OS squared = ON squared + SN squared.
  2. Magnitude R squared = (A + B cos theta) squared + (B sin theta) squared = A squared + B squared + 2AB cos theta.
  3. Direction R sin alpha = B sin theta, so sin alpha = B sin theta / R, where alpha is angle between R and A.

Answer R squared = A squared + B squared + 2AB cos theta; direction from sin alpha = B sin theta / R.

Worked example

A motorboat is racing towards north at 25 km/h and the water current is 10 km/h in the direction of 60 degrees east of south. Find the resultant velocity of the boat.

  1. Draw vectors vb is 25 km/h north, vc is 10 km/h at 60 degrees east of south. Angle between them in parallelogram is 120 degrees.
  2. Magnitude by law of cosines R squared = 25 squared + 10 squared + 2 x 25 x 10 x cos 120 degrees, giving R approx 21.8 km/h.
  3. Direction by law of sines sin f = vc sin 120 degrees / R = 10 x sin 120 degrees / 21.8 = 0.397, so f approx 23.4 degrees.

Answer Resultant speed about 21.8 km/h, direction given by f = 23.4 degrees in the source figure.

When does |a + b| = |a| + |b| hold as equality in the vector inequalities?

Why?

Geometrically the triangle inequality becomes equality only when vectors are collinear in the same direction. If a+b+c+d = 0 then |a+c| = |b+d| because a+c = -(b+d).

Position r equals x i plus y j. Displacement delta r equals r prime minus r equals delta x i plus delta y j. Average velocity is delta r over delta t.

Instantaneous velocity v equals dr over dt, tangent to the path. Acceleration a equals dv over dt. Components are vx equals dx over dt, vy equals dy over dt, ax equals dvx over dt, ay equals dvy over dt.

Velocity and acceleration as limits

Average velocity

v average = delta r / delta t, direction of delta r, which is a chord of the path.

Instantaneous velocity

As delta t tends to zero, chord becomes tangent; v = limit delta r / delta t = dr/dt.

Acceleration

a average = delta v / delta t; as delta t tends to zero, a = dv/dt. In 2D, a may make any angle 0 to 180 degrees with v.

Worked example

The position of a particle is given with coefficients 3.0, 2.0 and 5.0, t in seconds and r in metres. Find v(t) and a(t), and magnitude and direction of v at t = 1.0 s.

  1. Differentiate v = dr/dt = 3.0 i + 4.0 t j m/s in the source result; a = dv/dt = 4.0 j m/s squared.
  2. At t = 1.0 s v = 3.0 i + 4.0 j m/s.
  3. Magnitude and direction Magnitude = sqrt(3 squared + 4 squared) = 5.0 m/s; theta = tan inverse (4/3) = 53 degrees with x-axis.

Answer a = 4.0 m s-2 along y-direction; at 1.0 s, speed 5.0 m/s at 53 degrees with x-axis.

With constant acceleration, average acceleration equals that constant. From v0 at t=0 to v at t, v equals v0 plus a t. Position is r equals r0 plus v0 t plus one half a t squared.

Write x and y separately: vx equals vox plus ax t, x equals x0 plus vox t plus one half ax t squared, and similarly for y. Treat the two perpendicular motions independently.

Worked example

A particle starts from origin at t = 0 with velocity 5.0 i m/s and moves in x-y plane under constant acceleration (3.0 i + 2.0 j) m/s squared. What is y when x is 84 m, and what is speed then?

  1. Position x = 5.0 t + 1.5 t squared, y = 1.0 t squared from r = v0 t + one half a t squared.
  2. Find t 5.0 t + 1.5 t squared = 84 gives t = 6 s. Then y = 1.0 x 36 = 36.0 m.
  3. Velocity v = 5.0 i + 3.0 t i + 2.0 t j; at 6 s, vx = 23.0, vy = 12.0 m/s, speed = sqrt(23 squared + 12 squared) approx 26 m/s.

Answer y = 36.0 m at t = 6 s; speed approx 26 m/s.

For arbitrary motion in space, which relation is always true?

Why?

Average velocity is defined as displacement over time interval. The other forms hold only for constant acceleration, and a average = [v(t2)-v(t1)]/(t2-t1) is the definition for acceleration.

A projectile is in flight after being thrown, such as a ball. Its motion is two simultaneous components: horizontal without acceleration and vertical with constant g downward. Air resistance is neglected.

Choose x horizontal and y vertical up. Then ax equals 0 and ay equals -g. Initial components are vox equals v0 cos theta0 and voy equals v0 sin theta0.

Projectile trajectory

A projectile launched from origin with speed v0 at angle theta0. The curve is computed from x = v0 cos theta0 t and y = v0 sin theta0 t - 0.5 g t squared.

Projectile equations and key results

Position and velocity

x = v0 cos theta0 t, y = v0 sin theta0 t - 0.5 g t squared; vx = v0 cos theta0, vy = v0 sin theta0 - g t.

Path

Eliminate t: y = x tan theta0 - g x squared / (2 v0 squared cos squared theta0), a parabola.

Top and flight

vy = 0 gives tm = v0 sin theta0 / g; y=0 gives Tf = 2 tm; substituting tm gives hm = v0 squared sin squared theta0 / (2g).

Range

R = v0 cos theta0 x Tf = v0 squared sin 2theta0 / g, maximum v0 squared / g at 45 degrees.

Worked example

Galileo stated that for elevations which exceed or fall short of 45 degrees by equal amounts, the ranges are equal. Prove this statement.

  1. Range formula R = v0 squared sin 2theta0 / g for launch speed v0.
  2. Two angles For 45 degrees + alpha and 45 degrees - alpha, 2theta0 is 90 degrees + 2alpha and 90 degrees - 2alpha.
  3. Sines equal sin(90 degrees + 2alpha) = sin(90 degrees - 2alpha) = cos 2alpha, so R is the same for both.

Answer Ranges are equal for elevations 45 degrees plus or minus alpha.

Worked example

A hiker stands on the edge of a cliff 490 m above the ground and throws a stone horizontally with initial speed 15 m s-1. Neglect air resistance, take g = 9.8 m s-2. Find time to ground and speed on hitting ground.

  1. Set axes Origin at cliff edge, x along initial velocity, y up. vox = 15 m/s, voy = 0, ay = -9.8 m/s squared.
  2. Time Ground when y = -490 m: -490 = -0.5 x 9.8 x t squared, giving t = 10 s.
  3. Speed vx = 15 m/s, vy = -9.8 x 10 = -98 m/s, speed = sqrt(15 squared + 98 squared) approx 99 m/s.

Answer Time 10 s; speed approx 99 m/s on hitting ground.

Worked example

A cricket ball is thrown at 28 m s-1 in a direction 30 degrees above the horizontal. Calculate maximum height, time to return to same level, and distance to that point.

  1. Maximum height hm = v0 squared sin squared 30 degrees / (2g) = 28 squared x 0.25 / (2 x 9.8) = 10.0 m.
  2. Time of flight Tf = 2 v0 sin 30 degrees / g = 2 x 28 x 0.5 / 9.8 = 2.9 s.
  3. Range R = v0 squared sin 60 degrees / g = 28 squared x sin 60 degrees / 9.8 approx 69 m.

Answer Maximum height 10.0 m; time 2.9 s; distance 69 m.

A cricketer can throw a ball to a maximum horizontal distance of 100 m. How high above the ground can the cricketer throw the same ball?

Why?

Rmax = v0 squared / g = 100 m, so v0 squared = 100 g. Maximum height for same v0 is v0 squared / (2g) = 50 m. A ceiling limits height in the same way by limiting vertical component.

Uniform circular motion means following a circle at constant speed. Velocity direction changes continuously, so acceleration exists and points towards the centre.

Magnitude is ac equals v squared over R. Direction changes at every point, so acceleration is not a constant vector even though magnitude is constant.

Angular speed and centripetal acceleration

Angular speed

omega = delta theta / delta t, rate of change of angular displacement.

Linear speed

Distance delta s = R delta theta, so v = delta s / delta t = R omega.

Acceleration forms

ac = v squared / R = omega squared R. With period T and frequency n = 1/T, v = 2 pi R / T = 2 pi R n, omega = 2 pi n, ac = 4 pi squared n squared R.

Worked example

An insect trapped in a circular groove of radius 12 cm moves steadily and completes 7 revolutions in 100 s. Find angular speed, linear speed, and magnitude of acceleration. Is acceleration a constant vector?

  1. Angular speed omega = 2 pi x 7 / 100 = 0.44 rad/s.
  2. Linear speed v = omega R = 0.44 x 12 cm = 5.3 cm/s, tangent to circle.
  3. Acceleration a = omega squared R = 0.44 squared x 12 = 2.3 cm/s squared towards centre; direction changes, so not constant vector.

Answer omega = 0.44 rad/s, v = 5.3 cm/s, a = 2.3 cm/s squared towards centre, not constant vector.

A stone tied to a string 80 cm long is whirled in a horizontal circle with constant speed, making 14 revolutions in 25 s. What is magnitude and direction of acceleration?

Why?

omega = 2 pi x 14 / 25 = 3.52 rad/s; ac = omega squared R = 12.4 x 0.80 approx 9.9 m/s squared, directed to centre.

An aircraft executes a horizontal loop of radius 1.00 km with steady speed 900 km/h. Compare its centripetal acceleration with g.

Why?

900 km/h = 250 m/s; ac = v squared / R = 250 squared / 1000 = 62.5 m/s squared; divided by 9.8 gives about 6.4 g.

Path length depends on actual path; displacement depends only on end points. They are equal only if direction does not change. Average speed is therefore greater than or equal to magnitude of average velocity.

Vector equations for constant acceleration do not need a choice of axes, but you may resolve them along any two independent axes. Trajectory depends on initial position and velocity as well as acceleration. Resultant velocity is v1 plus v2, while relative velocity v12 is v1 minus v2 with respect to a common frame.

Three girls start at P on a circular ground of radius 200 m and reach Q diametrically opposite by different paths. What is magnitude of displacement for each?

Why?

Displacement is straight line PQ, the diameter 400 m, same for all. Path length equals displacement only for the girl who goes straight along the diameter.

A hotel is 10 km away on a straight road. A cab takes a 23 km circuitous path and reaches in 28 min. Find average speed and magnitude of average velocity.

Why?

Average speed = 23 km / (28/60 h) approx 49 km/h. Magnitude of average velocity = 10 km / (28/60 h) approx 21 km/h. Path length differs from displacement, so they differ.

What are magnitude and direction of i + j and i - j?

Why?

i+j has components 1,1 magnitude sqrt(2) direction tan inverse 1 = 45 degrees. i-j has components 1,-1 magnitude sqrt(2) direction -45 degrees. Components along these directions can be found graphically by projection.

Which statement about a scalar quantity is false?

Why?

A scalar is defined by magnitude only; the source does not require it to be conserved, dimensionless, non-negative or constant in space. Temperature difference and other scalars show the definition is magnitude only.

Position is r = 3.0 t squared i - 2.0 t squared j + 4.0 t squared k metres, t in seconds. What is magnitude of velocity at t = 2.0 s?

Why?

v = dr/dt = 6.0 t i - 4.0 t j + 8.0 t k; at 2.0 s v = 12 i - 8 j + 16 k, magnitude sqrt(144+64+256)=sqrt(464) approx 21.5 m/s. Acceleration is 6 i -4 j +8 k m/s squared.

An aircraft flies at height 3400 m. Angle subtended at a ground point by positions 10.0 s apart is 30 degrees. Estimate speed, assuming the ground point is below the midpoint of the two positions.

Why?

Half distance = 3400 x tan 15 degrees approx 911 m, total distance approx 1822 m in 10.0 s, speed approx 182 m/s. This uses the midpoint assumption stated because figure geometry is not fully readable in source.

Glossary

scalar
Quantity with magnitude only, such as mass or time.
vector
Quantity with magnitude and direction, such as velocity.
displacement
Straight-line change in position from start to finish, independent of path.
unit vector
Vector of magnitude 1 used to specify direction only.
projectile
Object in flight after being thrown, with independent horizontal and vertical motion.
centripetal acceleration
Acceleration towards the centre of a circle, magnitude v squared over R.

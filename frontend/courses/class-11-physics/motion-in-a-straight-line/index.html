Motion in a Straight Line

After this lesson you can:

  • After this lesson you can describe motion along a straight line using position, velocity and acceleration.
  • After this lesson you can read velocity and acceleration from position-time and velocity-time graphs.
  • After this lesson you can use the kinematic equations for uniformly accelerated motion and for free fall.
  • After this lesson you can distinguish average speed from magnitude of average velocity and explain relative motion with a frame of reference.

You see motion all around you. You walk, you run, and you ride a bicycle. Leaves fall from trees and water flows down a dam.

Motion means change in position of an object with time. To describe it, you first choose an origin. Position to the right of the origin is taken as positive and to the left as negative.

In this lesson we study motion along a straight line. This is called rectilinear motion. We treat objects as point objects when their size is much smaller than the distance they move.

Kinematics is the study of ways to describe motion. It does not go into the causes of motion. We develop velocity and acceleration to describe how position changes with time.

A railway carriage moves without jerks between two stations, much farther than its own length. Can it be treated as a point object?

Why?

An object can be treated as a point object when its size is much smaller than the distance it moves in a reasonable time.

Average velocity tells you how fast an object moved over a whole interval. It does not tell you how fast it moved at one instant.

Instantaneous velocity is the limit of average velocity as the time interval becomes infinitesimally small. In symbols, v = dx/dt.

On a position-time graph, velocity at an instant is the slope of the tangent at that point. Smaller intervals make the joining line approach the tangent.

For x = 0.08 t^3, the average velocity centred at t = 4.0 s approaches 3.84 m s-1 as the interval decreases. That limiting value is the velocity at t = 4.0 s.

Position-time graph and tangent slope

Shows the curve x = 0.08 t^3 and the tangent at a chosen time. The slope of the tangent is the instantaneous velocity.

Worked example

The position of an object moving along x-axis is given by x = a + b t^2 where a = 8.5 m, b = 2.5 m s-2 and t is measured in seconds. What is its velocity at t = 0 s and t = 2.0 s? What is the average velocity between t = 2.0 s and t = 4.0 s?

  1. Differentiate position v = dx/dt = 2 b t = 5.0 t m s-1.
  2. Velocity at t = 0 s v = 5.0 x 0 = 0 m s-1.
  3. Velocity at t = 2.0 s v = 5.0 x 2.0 = 10 m s-1.
  4. Average velocity between 2.0 s and 4.0 s x at 2.0 s = 8.5 + 2.5 x 4 = 18.5 m. x at 4.0 s = 8.5 + 2.5 x 16 = 48.5 m. Average velocity = (48.5 - 18.5)/(4.0 - 2.0) = 15 m s-1.

Answer Velocity at t = 0 s is 0 m s-1, at t = 2.0 s is 10 m s-1, and average velocity between 2.0 s and 4.0 s is 15 m s-1.

Speed is the magnitude of velocity. A velocity of +24.0 m s-1 and a velocity of -24.0 m s-1 both have a speed of 24.0 m s-1.

Average speed over a finite interval is greater than or equal to the magnitude of average velocity. But instantaneous speed at an instant is equal to the magnitude of instantaneous velocity at that instant.

Velocity changes during motion. We describe this change as rate of change of velocity with time. This rate is acceleration.

Average acceleration is a = (v2 - v1)/(t2 - t1), with SI unit m s-2. Instantaneous acceleration is a = dv/dt, the slope of the tangent to the velocity-time curve.

Acceleration can be positive, negative or zero. It can come from a change in speed, a change in direction, or both.

Position-time graphs curve upward for positive acceleration, downward for negative acceleration, and are straight lines for zero acceleration.

Velocity-time graph and area as displacement

Shows a velocity-time line for constant acceleration and shades the area under it. The shaded area equals displacement.

If a particle is speeding up, acceleration is in the direction of velocity. If its speed is decreasing, acceleration is in the direction opposite to velocity.

This is true whatever origin and positive direction you choose. The sign of acceleration alone does not tell you if speed is increasing or decreasing.

For constant acceleration, average acceleration equals the constant value during the interval. If velocity is v0 at t = 0 and v at time t, then v = v0 + a t.

The area under the velocity-time graph is a triangle plus a rectangle. This area equals displacement.

This gives x = ((v0 + v)/2) t, so average velocity is the arithmetic average of initial and final velocities.

Eliminating t gives the three kinematic equations for uniformly accelerated motion.

Deriving kinematic equations from the velocity-time area

Start with v = v0 + a t

This comes from a = (v - v0)/t for constant acceleration.

Find area under v-t graph

Area = area of triangle + area of rectangle = (1/2)(v - v0) t + v0 t = ((v0 + v)/2) t.

Area equals displacement

So x = ((v0 + v)/2) t.

Eliminate t

Use t = (v - v0)/a in x = ((v0 + v)/2) t to get v^2 = v0^2 + 2 a x. Use v - v0 = a t in x = ((v0 + v)/2) t to get x = v0 t + (1/2) a t^2.

Deriving equations of motion by calculus

Integrate acceleration

a = dv/dt, so dv = a dt. Integrating gives v - v0 = a t, so v = v0 + a t.

Integrate velocity

v = dx/dt, so dx = v dt. Integrating with v = v0 + a t gives x - x0 = v0 t + (1/2) a t^2.

Eliminate time

From a = dv/dt and v = dx/dt, a = v dv/dx, so v dv = a dx. Integrating gives (1/2)(v^2 - v0^2) = a (x - x0), so v^2 = v0^2 + 2 a (x - x0).

Worked example

A ball is thrown vertically upwards with a velocity of 20 m s-1 from the top of a multistorey building. The height of the point from where the ball is thrown is 25.0 m from the ground. (a) How high will the ball rise? and (b) how long will it be before the ball hits the ground? Take g = 10 m s-2.

  1. Choose upward as positive v0 = +20 m s-1, a = -g = -10 m s-2, and at the top v = 0 m s-1.
  2. Height risen Use v^2 = v0^2 + 2 a (y - y0): 0 = (20)^2 + 2(-10)(y - y0), so y - y0 = 20 m. Height from ground is 25.0 + 20 = 45 m.
  3. Time to go up Use v = v0 + a t: 0 = 20 - 10 t1, so t1 = 2 s.
  4. Time to fall down From the top, v0 = 0, a = -10 m s-2, y0 = 45 m, y = 0: 0 = 45 + (1/2)(-10) t2^2, so t2 = 3 s. Total time = 2 + 3 = 5 s.

Answer (a) The ball rises 20 m above the launch point, 45 m from the ground. (b) It hits the ground after 5 s.

Free fall is motion under gravity with air resistance neglected. Near the earth's surface g is constant at 9.8 m s-2 when the height is small compared to the earth's radius.

Take upward as positive. Then acceleration due to gravity is a = -g = -9.8 m s-2.

For an object released from rest at y = 0, the kinematic equations give v = -9.8 t, y = -4.9 t^2 and v^2 = -19.6 y.

Acceleration is constant, velocity varies linearly with time, and distance varies as t^2.

Worked example

Discuss the motion of an object under free fall. Neglect air resistance. Take g = 9.8 m s-2, upward positive, released from rest at y = 0.

  1. Write acceleration a = -g = -9.8 m s-2, v0 = 0.
  2. Velocity with time v = v0 + a t = 0 - 9.8 t = -9.8 t m s-1.
  3. Position with time y = 0 + (1/2) a t^2 = -4.9 t^2 m.
  4. Velocity with position v^2 = 0 + 2 a y = -19.6 y m^2 s-2.

Answer v = -9.8 t m s-1, y = -4.9 t^2 m, v^2 = -19.6 y m^2 s-2.

Worked example

Galileo's law of odd numbers: The distances traversed, during equal intervals of time, by a body falling from rest, stand to one another in the same ratio as the odd numbers beginning with unity, namely 1:3:5:7. Prove it.

  1. Use free-fall position For fall from rest, position magnitude is (1/2) g t^2.
  2. Take equal intervals Divide time into equal intervals of length tau and find positions after tau, 2 tau, 3 tau and so on.
  3. Find distances in successive intervals Subtract successive positions. The distances are in the ratio 1:3:5:7:9:11 and so on.

Answer Distances in successive equal time intervals are in the ratio 1:3:5:7:9:11, proving the law of odd numbers.

Worked example

When brakes are applied to a moving vehicle, the distance it travels before stopping is called stopping distance. Derive an expression for stopping distance in terms of v0 and a, where a is the deceleration caused by braking.

  1. Use v^2 = v0^2 + 2 a x Let stopping distance be ds and final velocity v = 0.
  2. Solve for ds 0 = v0^2 + 2 a ds, so ds = -v0^2/(2 a).
  3. Interpret the result Stopping distance is proportional to v0^2. Doubling v0 increases ds by a factor of 4 for the same deceleration.

Answer Stopping distance ds = -v0^2/(2 a), proportional to the square of initial velocity.

Worked example

Reaction time is the time a person takes to observe, think and act. A ruler is dropped vertically and caught after travelling d = 21.0 cm. Estimate the reaction time. Take g = 9.8 m s-2.

  1. Use free fall The ruler drops from rest, so distance magnitude is (1/2) g t_r^2.
  2. Insert d d = 21.0 cm = 0.210 m, so 0.210 = (1/2) x 9.8 x t_r^2.
  3. Solve for t_r t_r^2 = 0.420/9.8 = 0.0429, so t_r = 0.21 s approximately.

Answer Reaction time is about 0.21 s.

For uniform motion, acceleration is zero. The x-t graph is a straight line inclined to the time axis and the v-t graph is a straight line parallel to the time axis.

For uniform acceleration, the x-t graph is a parabola and the v-t graph is a straight line inclined to the time axis.

Choose the origin and positive direction before assigning signs. The sign of acceleration alone does not tell whether speed increases or decreases.

Zero velocity at an instant does not mean zero acceleration. Kinematic equations apply only when acceleration magnitude and direction are constant, with values substituted with proper signs.

Which statement is true for one-dimensional motion?

Why?

A particle thrown up has zero velocity at the top but gravity acceleration continues. Zero speed means zero velocity magnitude, constant speed can still have acceleration from direction change, and positive acceleration depends on the chosen positive direction.

Worked example

A car moving along a straight highway with speed of 126 km h-1 is brought to a stop within a distance of 200 m. What is the retardation of the car (assumed uniform), and how long does it take for the car to stop?

  1. Convert speed 126 km h-1 = 35 m s-1. Final velocity v = 0, distance x = 200 m.
  2. Find retardation Use v^2 = v0^2 + 2 a x: 0 = (35)^2 + 2 a (200), so a = -3.06 m s-2. Retardation is 3.06 m s-2.
  3. Find time Use v = v0 + a t: 0 = 35 - 3.06 t, so t = 11.4 s approximately.

Answer Retardation is about 3.06 m s-2 and the car stops in about 11.4 s.

Worked example

A player throws a ball upwards with an initial speed of 29.4 m s-1. (a) What is the direction of acceleration during upward motion? (b) What are velocity and acceleration at the highest point? (d) To what height does the ball rise and after how long does it return to the player's hands? Take g = 9.8 m s-2 and neglect air resistance.

  1. Direction of acceleration Acceleration due to gravity is downward during upward motion.
  2. At highest point Velocity is zero, acceleration is still downward with magnitude 9.8 m s-2.
  3. Height risen Use v^2 = v0^2 + 2 a x with upward positive: 0 = (29.4)^2 + 2(-9.8) h, so h = 44.1 m.
  4. Time to return Time up = 29.4/9.8 = 3 s. By symmetry time down is also 3 s, so total time is 6 s.

Answer Acceleration is downward, velocity at the top is zero with downward acceleration 9.8 m s-2, height risen is 44.1 m, and return time is 6 s.

Worked example

A drunkard walking in a narrow lane takes 5 steps forward and 3 steps backward, followed again by 5 steps forward and 3 steps backward, and so on. Each step is 1 m long and requires 1 s. How long does he take to fall in a pit 13 m away from the start?

  1. Plot motion Each forward step has slope +1 m/s and each backward step has slope -1 m/s on an x-t graph.
  2. Track position After first 5 forward: 5 m at 5 s. After 3 back: 2 m at 8 s. After next 5 forward: 7 m at 13 s. After 3 back: 4 m at 16 s. After next 5 forward: 9 m at 21 s. After 3 back: 6 m at 24 s. After next 5 forward: 11 m at 29 s. After 3 back: 8 m at 32 s.
  3. Reach the pit Next 5 forward steps take him from 8 m to 13 m, reaching the pit at 37 s.

Answer He falls in the pit after 37 s.

A woman walks from home to an office 2.5 km away, stays at the office for a long time, then returns home by auto. What does the x-t graph look like during her stay at the office?

Why?

During the stay, position is constant, so the x-t graph is horizontal and its slope, velocity, is zero. Walking to the office gives a positive slope and returning gives a negative slope.

Worked example

A man walks on a straight road from his home to a market 2.5 km away with a speed of 5 km h-1. Finding the market closed, he instantly turns and walks back home with a speed of 7.5 km h-1. What is the magnitude of average velocity and average speed over (i) 0 to 30 min, (ii) 0 to 50 min, (iii) 0 to 40 min?

  1. Time to market 2.5 km at 5 km h-1 takes 0.5 h = 30 min.
  2. 0 to 30 min Displacement = 2.5 km, path = 2.5 km. Magnitude of average velocity = 2.5/0.5 = 5 km h-1. Average speed = 5 km h-1.
  3. 0 to 50 min In 20 min back at 7.5 km h-1 he covers 2.5 km and reaches home. Displacement = 0, so magnitude of average velocity = 0. Path = 5 km in 50 min, average speed = 6 km h-1.
  4. 0 to 40 min In 10 min back he covers 1.25 km, so position from home is 1.25 km. Magnitude of average velocity = 1.25/(2/3) = 1.875 km h-1. Path = 3.75 km, average speed = 3.75/(2/3) = 5.625 km h-1.

Answer (i) 5 km h-1 and 5 km h-1. (ii) 0 km h-1 and 6 km h-1. (iii) 1.875 km h-1 and 5.625 km h-1.

For one-dimensional motion, how do magnitude of displacement and total path length compare over an interval?

Why?

Displacement depends only on start and end positions, while path length adds every step. Turning back makes path length larger. The same holds for average speed versus magnitude of average velocity.

Two children A and B return from school on x-t graphs. A starts at t = 0 with a smaller slope, B starts later with a larger slope, and both end at the same home position. Which conclusion follows?

Why?

Slope of an x-t graph is velocity. Larger slope means larger speed. The intercept on the time axis shows start time, and the final position shows distance from school.

A ball is dropped from a height of 90 m and at each collision with the floor loses one tenth of its speed. What will its speed-time graph between t = 0 and 12 s look like?

Why?

During free fall speed increases with time. At collision speed falls to nine tenths, so the next peak is smaller. Velocity and acceleration cannot change abruptly in a realistic smooth graph, but speed drops at the bounce in this idealised description.

Which of these cannot possibly represent one-dimensional motion of a particle?

Why?

A vertical x-t segment would mean position changes in zero time, giving infinite velocity. Straight inclined x-t, inclined v-t and parabolic x-t are all possible for uniform or uniformly accelerated motion.

An x-t graph is a straight line for t < 0 and a parabola for t > 0. Is it correct to say the particle moves in a straight line for t < 0 and on a parabolic path for t > 0?

Why?

An x-t graph is not a trajectory in space. For one-dimensional motion the particle moves on a straight line; a parabolic x-t graph indicates constant acceleration.

Motion is always measured with respect to a fixed point. This fixed point is called a frame of reference.

The choice of point is arbitrary and all frames of reference are equally valid. A passenger in a moving car is not moving with respect to the driver.

Both passenger and driver are moving for a person on the sidewalk. Relative velocity compares motion seen from different frames.

To find the speed relevant for one object hitting another, you compare their velocities in the same frame of reference.

Worked example

A police van moving on a highway with a speed of 30 km h-1 fires a bullet at a thief's car speeding away in the same direction with a speed of 192 km h-1. If the muzzle speed of the bullet is 150 m s-1, with what speed does the bullet hit the thief's car?

  1. Convert speeds to m s-1 30 km h-1 = 8.33 m s-1 and 192 km h-1 = 53.33 m s-1.
  2. Bullet speed with respect to ground Bullet speed = muzzle speed + van speed = 150 + 8.33 = 158.33 m s-1 in the same direction.
  3. Speed relative to thief's car Relative speed = 158.33 - 53.33 = 105 m s-1. This is the speed relevant for damaging the thief's car.

Answer The bullet hits the thief's car with a relative speed of about 105 m s-1.

A passenger sits in a moving car. With respect to whom is the passenger moving?

Why?

Motion is relative to a frame of reference. The passenger and driver share the same frame, while the sidewalk person is in a different frame.

A velocity-time graph rises with a constant slope, then becomes horizontal, then falls with a constant slope to zero. What physical situation does it suggest?

Why?

Slope of v-t graph is acceleration. Rising slope means positive acceleration, horizontal means zero acceleration, and falling slope means negative acceleration while still moving forward until velocity reaches zero.

On an x-t graph at a chosen instant, position is positive and the tangent slope is negative. What are the signs of position and velocity at that instant?

Why?

Position sign is read from the value of x. Velocity sign is read from the slope of the tangent; a negative slope means velocity is negative.

An x-t graph shows three equal time intervals. The middle interval has the steepest slope, the first interval has a moderate slope, and the last interval has the shallowest slope, all with positive slope. In which interval is average speed greatest and least?

Why?

Average speed over an interval is magnitude of displacement divided by time, which for a straight segment is the magnitude of slope. Steepest slope gives greatest average speed and shallowest gives least. All average velocities are positive because slopes are positive.

A speed-time graph for motion in a constant direction shows three equal time intervals. The first interval has the steepest rising slope, the second is horizontal, and the third has a shallow falling slope. In which interval is average acceleration greatest in magnitude and average speed greatest?

Why?

Average acceleration is slope of speed-time graph, so steepest slope gives greatest magnitude. Average speed is average height of the graph over the interval, so the interval with highest speed level gives greatest average speed. With positive direction as motion direction, velocity is positive in all intervals and acceleration is positive, zero, then negative.

Remember the three kinematic equations: v = v0 + a t, x = v0 t + (1/2) a t^2, and v^2 = v0^2 + 2 a x.

Use (x - x0) if the particle starts at x0. Substitute values with proper signs after choosing origin and positive direction.

For free fall with upward positive, use a = -9.8 m s-2. For stopping, remember distance grows as the square of initial velocity.

Read graphs carefully: slope of x-t is velocity, slope of v-t is acceleration, and area under v-t is displacement.

An object falls freely from rest. Using y = -4.9 t^2 with upward positive, what is the magnitude of distance fallen in 2 s? Give the answer in metres.

Why?

Magnitude of y = 4.9 x (2)^2 = 4.9 x 4 = 19.6 m. The negative sign shows displacement is downward when upward is positive.

Why is there no distinction between instantaneous speed and magnitude of instantaneous velocity, while average speed can differ from magnitude of average velocity?

Why?

Instantaneous speed is defined as the magnitude of instantaneous velocity. Average speed uses total path length over a finite interval, which can exceed the magnitude of average velocity when direction changes.

A particle is speeding up. What is the direction of its acceleration relative to its velocity?

Why?

If a particle is speeding up, acceleration is in the direction of velocity. If speed is decreasing, acceleration is opposite to velocity. This is independent of the choice of origin and axis.

Glossary

Position
Location of an object with respect to a chosen origin; to the right is positive and to the left is negative for motion in a straight line.
Velocity
Rate of change of position with time at an instant, v = dx/dt; it has magnitude and direction.
Acceleration
Rate of change of velocity with time, a = dv/dt; it has magnitude and direction and its unit is m s-2.
Displacement
Change in position over a time interval; it can be positive, negative or zero and equals the area under the velocity-time graph.
Average speed
Total path length divided by the time interval; it is greater than or equal to the magnitude of average velocity.
Frame of reference
The fixed point or group of points with respect to which motion is measured.

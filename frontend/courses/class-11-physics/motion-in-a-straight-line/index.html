Motion in a Straight Line

After this lesson you can:

  • After this lesson you can define velocity and acceleration as rates of change and read them from graphs.
  • After this lesson you can use the three kinematic equations for uniformly accelerated motion with correct signs.
  • After this lesson you can solve free fall, stopping distance, reaction time and relative velocity problems from this chapter.
  • After this lesson you can distinguish displacement from path length and average velocity from average speed.

You see motion everywhere. Motion is change in position of an object with time. In this lesson we study only motion along a straight line, called rectilinear motion.

We treat objects as point objects when size is much smaller than distance moved. Kinematics describes motion without going into its causes.

In which examples can the body be considered approximately a point object? (a) railway carriage moving without jerks (b) monkey on man cycling on circular track (c) spinning cricket ball turning on ground (d) tumbling beaker slipping off table

Why?

Point object approximation is valid when size is much smaller than distance moved. A carriage moving without jerks between stations can be treated so; others involve size, rotation or tumbling.

Average velocity tells how fast an object moved over an interval. Instantaneous velocity is the limit of average velocity as time interval becomes infinitesimally small: v = dx/dt.

On a position-time graph, velocity at an instant is slope of tangent at that point. For x = 0.08 t3 at t = 4.0 s, average velocity approaches 3.84 m s-1 as interval shrinks.

Worked example

Example 2.1: Position x = a + b t2 where a = 8.5 m, b = 2.5 m s-2. Find velocity at t = 0 s and t = 2.0 s, and average velocity between t = 2.0 s and t = 4.0 s.

  1. Velocity formula v = dx/dt = 2 b t = 5.0 t m s-1
  2. At t=0 and t=2 v=0 m s-1 at 0 s; v=10 m s-1 at 2.0 s
  3. Average velocity (x(4)-x(2))/(4-2) = 15 m s-1

Answer 0 m s-1, 10 m s-1, 15 m s-1

Speed is magnitude of velocity. +24.0 m s-1 and -24.0 m s-1 both have speed 24.0 m s-1.

Average speed over a finite interval is greater than or equal to magnitude of average velocity. Instantaneous speed equals magnitude of instantaneous velocity.

Why is no distinction needed between instantaneous speed and magnitude of instantaneous velocity, but distinction is needed for average speed and magnitude of average velocity?

Why?

Average speed uses total path length, average velocity uses displacement. Instantaneously there is no interval for reversal, so speed is magnitude of velocity.

Average acceleration a = (v2 - v1)/(t2 - t1), SI unit m s-2. On velocity-time plot it is slope of line joining the two points.

Instantaneous acceleration a = dv/dt is slope of tangent to v-t curve. Acceleration can be positive, negative or zero and can arise from change in speed, direction, or both.

A particle in one-dimensional motion: (a) with zero speed at an instant may have non-zero acceleration (b) with zero speed may have non-zero velocity (c) with constant speed must have zero acceleration (d) with positive acceleration must be speeding up. Which are true?

Why?

(a) true: zero velocity at highest point but gravity acceleration continues. (b) false: speed is magnitude of velocity. (c) false in general if direction changes, and in 1D constant speed with reversal needs acceleration. (d) false: sign depends on positive direction choice.

Position-time graphs curve upward for positive acceleration, downward for negative, straight for zero. For uniform motion x-t is inclined straight line, v-t parallel to time axis. For uniform acceleration x-t is parabola, v-t inclined straight line.

Area under v-t curve between t1 and t2 equals displacement. For constant velocity u, area between 0 and T is uT.

On an x-t graph, how do you decide which interval has greatest average speed and sign of average velocity? And on a speed-time graph, which interval has greatest average acceleration magnitude?

Why?

Average velocity is slope of x-t joining line; average speed uses magnitude. Average acceleration is slope of v-t joining line.

Deriving kinematic equations from area under v-t graph

Start with v = v0 + a t

For constant a, v-t graph is straight line from v0 at t=0 to v at t.

Area = triangle + rectangle

Area between 0 and t = (1/2)(v - v0)t + v0 t = (1/2)(v+v0)t

Area = displacement

x = (1/2)(v+v0)t, and with v - v0 = a t get x = v0 t + (1/2) a t2 and v2 = v02 + 2 a x

Deriving equations by calculus (Example 2.2)

Integrate a = dv/dt

dv = a dt, integrate from v0 to v and 0 to t gives v = v0 + a t

Integrate v = dx/dt

dx = v dt gives x = x0 + v0 t + (1/2) a t2

Eliminate t

v dv = a dx gives v2 = v02 + 2a(x - x0). Method also works for non-uniform acceleration.

The three equations for x0=0 are v = v0 + a t, x = v0 t + (1/2) a t2, v2 = v02 + 2 a x. With x0 non-zero replace x by x - x0.

Choose origin and positive direction first, then substitute with proper signs. Kinematic equations need constant acceleration; definitions of velocity and acceleration are always exact.

Worked example

Example 2.3: Ball thrown vertically upwards with velocity 20 m s-1 from top of building 25.0 m from ground. Take g = 10 m s-2. (a) How high will ball rise? (b) How long before it hits ground?

  1. Set axes Upward positive, v0=+20 m s-1, a=-10 m s-2, v=0 at top
  2. Height rise 0 = 20^2 + 2(-10)(y-y0) gives y-y0=20 m
  3. Time Up: 0=20-10 t1 gives t1=2 s. Down from 45 m: 0=45-5 t2^2 gives t2=3 s. Total 5 s. Second method: 0=25+20t-5t^2 gives t=5 s.

Answer Rises 20 m above launch; total time 5 s

Worked example

Example 2.4: Discuss free fall neglecting air resistance, g = 9.8 m s-2, released from rest at y=0, upward positive.

  1. Acceleration a = -g = -9.8 m s-2, v0=0
  2. Equations v = -9.8 t m s-1, y = -4.9 t2 m, v2 = -19.6 y m2 s-2

Answer v = -9.8 t, y = -4.9 t2, v2 = -19.6 y

Worked example

Example 2.5: Prove Galileo's law of odd numbers for body falling from rest during equal intervals tau.

  1. Position after intervals Use y = -(1/2) g t2 for t = tau, 2tau, 3tau...
  2. Distances in successive intervals Differences give ratio 1:3:5:7:9:11...

Answer Distances in ratio 1:3:5:7...

Worked example

Example 2.6: Derive stopping distance in terms of v0 and deceleration -a. Braking distances 10 m, 20 m, 34 m, 50 m for 11, 15, 20, 25 m/s.

  1. Use v2 = v02 + 2 a x v=0 at stop, a negative
  2. Result ds = -v02/(2a), proportional to v02; doubling v0 gives factor 4

Answer ds = -v02/(2a)

Worked example

Example 2.7: Ruler dropped vertically, caught after falling d = 21.0 cm. Estimate reaction time, g = 9.8 m s-2.

  1. Free fall v0=0, a=-g, d = (1/2) g tr^2 in magnitude
  2. Solve tr = sqrt(2d/g) with d=0.210 m gives about 0.21 s

Answer Reaction time about 0.21 s from d=21.0 cm

A car moving at 126 km h-1 is brought to stop within 200 m. What is the magnitude of uniform retardation in m s-2?

Why?

Convert 126 km h-1 to 35 m s-1. Use v2=v02+2ax with v=0, x=200 m to get a magnitude 3.06 m s-2. Time to stop from v=v0+at is about 11.4 s. This uses only kinematic equations from source.

Player throws ball upwards with initial speed 29.4 m s-1, g=9.8 m s-2. (a) Direction of acceleration upward? (b) Velocity and acceleration at highest point? (d) Height rise and return time?

Why?

Gravity acceleration is downward even upward. At top velocity zero but acceleration -g continues. Height from v2=v02+2ax and time from v=v0+at.

A woman walks 2.5 km at 5 km h-1, stays till 5.00 pm, returns at 25 km h-1. Her x-t graph is inclined, then horizontal, then steeper inclined.

A drunkard takes 5 steps forward and 3 steps backward, each 1 m and 1 s. His x-t graph is saw-tooth rising overall; pit at 13 m is reached after counting cycles.

Man walks 2.5 km at 5 km h-1 to market, returns at 7.5 km h-1. What are magnitude of average velocity and average speed over 0-30, 0-50, 0-40 min?

Why?

At 30 min he is at market, at 40 min on way back, at 50 min home. Displacement at home is zero so average velocity zero, but total path is 5.0 km so average speed is non-zero.

Ball dropped from 90 m loses one tenth of its speed at each collision. What does speed-time graph 0 to 12 s look like?

Why?

Between bounces speed increases linearly with slope g. At each collision speed drops to nine tenths of previous impact speed.

Relative velocity: police van and thief car

Van at 30 km h-1, thief car at 192 km h-1 same direction, bullet muzzle speed 150 m s-1 relative to van.

Police van 30 km h-1 fires bullet muzzle 150 m s-1 at thief car 192 km h-1 same direction. With what speed in m s-1 does bullet hit thief car relative to the car?

Why?

Convert van 30 km h-1 to 8.33 m s-1 and car 192 km h-1 to 53.33 m s-1. Bullet relative to ground = 150+8.33=158.33 m s-1. Relative to car = 158.33-53.33=105 m s-1. That relative speed damages car.

If speeding up, acceleration is in direction of velocity; if slowing, opposite. Sign of acceleration does not tell if speed increases; it depends on positive direction.

Zero velocity does not mean zero acceleration. Kinematic equations are algebraic with signs; they apply only for constant acceleration.

Which graphs cannot represent one-dimensional motion, and can an x-t graph be straight for t<0 and parabolic for t>0 as a physical motion?

Why?

x-t graph is position vs time, not spatial path. Straight then parabolic means uniform then uniformly accelerated motion along line. Graphs implying abrupt velocity change or multiple positions at same time are impossible; realistic graphs are smooth.

For a particle in one-dimensional simple harmonic motion x-t plot, what are signs of position, velocity and acceleration at t=0.3 s, 1.2 s, -1.2 s? Use slope for velocity and curvature for acceleration.

Why?

Position is x coordinate, velocity is slope, acceleration is curvature of x-t graph. This is the method for reading signs from a graph without inventing figure values beyond reading signs.

Glossary

Velocity
Rate of change of position with time, dx/dt, with magnitude and direction.
Acceleration
Rate of change of velocity with time, dv/dt, SI unit m s-2.
Displacement
Change in position from start to end, can be positive, negative or zero.
Average speed
Total path length divided by time interval.
Free fall
Fall under gravity with air resistance neglected, uniform acceleration -g.

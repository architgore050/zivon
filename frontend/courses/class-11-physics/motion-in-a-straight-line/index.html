Motion in a Straight Line

After this lesson you can:

  • After this lesson you can define position, velocity, speed and acceleration and get them from graphs.
  • After this lesson you can use the three kinematic equations for uniform acceleration with correct signs.
  • After this lesson you can solve free fall, stopping distance, reaction time and relative velocity problems.
  • After this lesson you can distinguish displacement from path length and average velocity from average speed.

Motion is change in position of an object with time. You walk, run, ride a bicycle, leaves fall, water flows, earth rotates and revolves. In this lesson we study only motion along a straight line, called rectilinear motion.

We treat objects as point objects when size is much smaller than distance moved in reasonable time. Kinematics describes motion without going into causes.

In which can body be considered approximately a point object? (a) railway carriage moving without jerks (b) monkey on man cycling on circular track (c) spinning cricket ball turning on ground (d) tumbling beaker slipping off table

Why?

Point object needs size much smaller than distance moved and no important rotation or tumbling. Railway carriage moving without jerks qualifies; others involve rotation, spinning or tumbling where size matters.

Average velocity tells how fast over an interval, not at an instant. Instantaneous velocity v is limit of average velocity as Delta t becomes infinitesimally small: v = lim Delta x/Delta t = dx/dt.

On position-time graph, velocity at t=4 s is slope of tangent at point P. Secant P1P2 for Delta t=2 s, then Q1Q2 for Delta t=1 s, in limit becomes tangent.

Limiting process with x = 0.08 t^3 at t = 4.0 s

Centre interval at 4.0 s

Take Delta t = 2.0 s, 1.0 s, 0.5 s, 0.1 s, 0.01 s centred at t=4.0 s, so t1 = t - Delta t/2, t2 = t + Delta t/2.

Compute Delta x/Delta t

x(t1)=0.08 t1^3, x(t2)=0.08 t2^3, Delta x = x(t2)-x(t1), ratio is average velocity for that Delta t.

Take limit

As Delta t decreases from 2.0 s to 0.010 s, ratio approaches 3.84 m s-1, which is dx/dt at t=4.0 s.

Worked example

Position x = a + b t^2 where a = 8.5 m, b = 2.5 m s-2, t in seconds. Find velocity at t=0 s and t=2.0 s, and average velocity between t=2.0 s and t=4.0 s.

  1. Differentiate v = dx/dt = 2 b t = 5.0 t m s-1.
  2. At t=0 and 2.0 s v=0 m s-1 at 0 s; v=10 m s-1 at 2.0 s.
  3. Average velocity 2 to 4 s (x(4)-x(2))/(4-2) = (a+16b - (a+4b))/2 = 6.0 b = 15 m s-1.

Answer v(0)=0 m s-1, v(2)=10 m s-1, average velocity 2-4 s = 15 m s-1. For uniform motion velocity equals average velocity at all instants.

Why is instantaneous speed always equal to magnitude of instantaneous velocity, while average speed can be greater than magnitude of average velocity?

Why?

Over finite interval particle may turn back, making total path larger than net displacement. At an instant no turning interval exists, so magnitudes match.

Velocity changes with time. Galileo found rate of change of velocity with time is constant for free fall, while with distance it decreases. So acceleration is rate of change of velocity with time.

Average acceleration a = (v2-v1)/(t2-t1), unit m s-2. On v-t plot it is slope of line joining (v1,t1) and (v2,t2). Instantaneous acceleration is dv/dt, slope of tangent to v-t curve.

Acceleration can be from change in speed, direction, or both. It can be positive, negative or zero. x-t curves upward for positive, downward for negative, straight for zero.

For constant velocity u, v-t graph is line parallel to time axis. Area between 0 and T is rectangle u x T = uT, which is displacement. Dimensions of v (length/time) times t (time) give length.

Graphs may have sharp kinks in textbook figures, but realistic velocity and acceleration change continuously, not abruptly.

Derivation of kinematic equations from v-t area

Start with v = v0 + a t

For constant a, v = v0 + a t, graph is straight line from v0 at t=0 to v at t.

Area under curve

Area = triangle + rectangle = (1/2)(v - v0) t + v0 t = (1/2)(v+v0) t. This area is displacement x.

Eliminate t

Use v - v0 = a t to get x = v0 t + (1/2) a t^2. Substitute t=(v-v0)/a to get v^2 = v0^2 + 2 a x.

General x0

Replace x by x-x0: x = x0 + v0 t + (1/2) a t^2, v^2 = v0^2 + 2a(x-x0).

Equations by calculus

Integrate acceleration

a = dv/dt, dv = a dt, integrate from v0 to v and 0 to t gives v = v0 + a t for constant a.

Integrate velocity

v = dx/dt, dx = v dt, integrate gives x = x0 + v0 t + (1/2) a t^2.

Eliminate t

dv/dt = v dv/dx = a, so v dv = a dx, integrate gives v^2 = v0^2 + 2a(x-x0). Advantage: method also works for non-uniform acceleration.

Worked example

Ball thrown vertically up with 20 m s-1 from top of building 25.0 m from ground. Take g = 10 m s-2. (a) How high will ball rise? (b) How long before it hits ground?

  1. Choose upward positive v0=+20 m s-1, a=-g=-10 m s-2, at top v=0.
  2. Height 0 = 20^2 + 2(-10)(y-y0) gives y-y0 = 20 m above launch, 45 m from ground.
  3. Time first method Up: 0=20-10 t1 gives t1=2 s. Down from 45 m: 0=45+(1/2)(-10) t2^2 gives t2=3 s. Total 5 s.
  4. Time second method 0=25+20t+(1/2)(-10)t^2 gives 5t^2-20t-25=0, t=5 s. Second method avoids splitting path.

Answer (a) 20 m above launch point. (b) 5 s total.

Worked example

Discuss free fall neglecting air resistance, g = 9.8 m s-2, upward positive, released from rest at y=0.

  1. Acceleration a = -g = -9.8 m s-2 constant, so uniform acceleration.
  2. Velocity v = 0 - g t = -9.8 t m s-1.
  3. Position and v^2 y = - (1/2) g t^2 = -4.9 t^2 m; v^2 = -2 g y = -19.6 y m^2 s-2.

Answer v=-9.8t m s-1, y=-4.9t^2 m, v^2=-19.6y. Acceleration constant, velocity linear in t, distance quadratic in t.

Galileo law of odd numbers

Divide time into equal tau

Falling from rest, y = -(1/2) g t^2. Compute positions after tau, 2tau, 3tau...

Take unit y0

Let y0 = -(1/2) g tau^2 be position after first interval. Positions in units of y0 are 1,4,9,16...

Differences

Distances in successive tau intervals are 1,3,5,7,9,11... in units of y0, ratio 1:3:5:7...

Worked example

Derive stopping distance in terms of v0 and deceleration -a. Braking distances for a car were 10 m, 20 m, 34 m, 50 m for velocities 11, 15, 20, 25 m/s.

  1. Use v^2 = v0^2 + 2 a x At stop v=0, distance ds, so 0 = v0^2 + 2(-a) ds.
  2. Solve ds = -v0^2/(2a) with a negative, magnitude v0^2/(2|a|).
  3. Check proportionality ds proportional to v0^2. Doubling v0 increases ds by factor 4. Given data nearly consistent.

Answer ds = v0^2/(2|a|), proportional to square of initial velocity.

Worked example

Ruler dropped vertically through thumb and forefinger, caught after falling d = 21.0 cm. Estimate reaction time, g = 9.8 m s-2.

  1. Free fall v0=0, a=-g, distance d = (1/2) g t_r^2 in magnitude.
  2. Solve for t_r t_r = sqrt(2d/g) = sqrt(2 x 0.210 / 9.8) s.
  3. Estimate t_r approx 0.21 s from formula; reaction time is time to observe, think and act.

Answer Reaction time from d=21.0 cm is sqrt(2d/g), about 0.2 s.

Summary: velocity is slope of tangent to x-t graph. Acceleration is slope of v-t graph. Area under v-t between t1 and t2 is displacement.

For uniform motion a=0, x-t straight inclined, v-t straight parallel to time axis. For uniform acceleration x-t parabola, v-t straight inclined.

Choose origin and positive direction first. Speeding up means acceleration along velocity; slowing means opposite. Sign of acceleration alone does not tell speeding up or slowing. Zero velocity does not mean zero acceleration.

Worked example

Police van 30 km h-1 fires bullet at thief car 192 km h-1 same direction. Muzzle speed 150 m s-1. With what speed does bullet hit thief car?

  1. Convert muzzle speed 150 m s-1 = 540 km h-1 relative to van.
  2. Bullet speed relative ground 540 + 30 = 570 km h-1 in same direction.
  3. Relative to thief car 570 - 192 = 378 km h-1. This relative speed is relevant for damaging thief car.

Answer 378 km h-1 relative to thief car (570 km h-1 relative to ground).

State true/false with reason: (a) zero speed may have non-zero acceleration (b) zero speed may have non-zero velocity (c) constant speed must have zero acceleration (d) positive acceleration must be speeding up

Why?

(a) True at top of throw. (b) False speed is magnitude of velocity. (c) False acceleration can be direction change. (d) False sign depends on axis choice.

For x-t graphs of A and B from school O to homes P and Q, which graph is steeper means faster, earlier start means line leaves origin earlier, closer home means smaller final x. Which entries follow?

Why?

Slope of x-t is velocity, so steeper is faster. Where line starts on t-axis is start time. Final x is home distance. Apply to the graph to choose A/B for each subpart including overtaking and same/different arrival.

Plotting: Woman 2.5 km at 5 km h-1, stay till 5 pm, return 25 km h-1; drunkard 5 forward 3 back steps 1 m 1 s each to pit 13 m. What shape x-t?

Why?

x-t slope is velocity. Staying means slope zero horizontal. Return means negative slope steeper magnitude. Drunkard forward steps slope positive, backward negative, net forward, time to 13 m found graphically and by counting cycles.

Worked example

Car moving along straight highway with speed 126 km h-1 is brought to stop within 200 m. What is retardation (uniform) and how long to stop?

  1. Convert speed 126 km h-1 = 35 m s-1.
  2. Use v^2 = v0^2 + 2 a x 0 = 35^2 + 2 a (200) gives a = -1225/400 = -3.06 m s-2 approx.
  3. Time 0 = 35 + a t gives t = 35/3.06 approx 11.4 s, or average velocity 17.5 m s-1 over 200 m.

Answer Retardation about 3.1 m s-2, time about 11 s.

Worked example

Player throws ball up with 29.4 m s-1, g=9.8 m s-2, neglect air. Height and return time? Direction of acceleration upward? Velocity and acceleration at highest point?

  1. Acceleration direction Gravity downward throughout, so during upward motion acceleration is downward, negative if up is positive.
  2. Highest point Velocity zero, acceleration still -g downward.
  3. Height 0 = 29.4^2 + 2(-9.8) h gives h = 44.1 m.
  4. Return time Up time 29.4/9.8=3 s, total return 6 s by symmetry.

Answer Height 44.1 m, return after 6 s; at top v=0, a=-9.8 m s-2 downward.

Distinguish magnitude of displacement vs total path length, and magnitude of average velocity vs average speed. When is second equal to first?

Why?

Path length counts all movement, displacement is net change. Average speed = path/time, magnitude of average velocity = |net displacement|/time, so speed >= that magnitude, equality only for one-way motion.

Worked example

Man walks 2.5 km at 5 km h-1 to market, instantly turns back at 7.5 km h-1. Find magnitude of average velocity and average speed over (i) 0-30 min (ii) 0-50 min (iii) 0-40 min.

  1. Times To market 0.5 h =30 min. Back 2.5 km at 7.5 km h-1 takes 20 min, home at 50 min.
  2. 0-30 min Displacement 2.5 km, path 2.5 km, avg velocity magnitude = average speed =5 km h-1.
  3. 0-50 min Displacement 0, path 5.0 km, avg velocity 0, average speed =5.0 km / (50/60 h)=6 km h-1.
  4. 0-40 min 10 min back =1.25 km back, displacement 1.25 km, path 3.75 km, avg vel =1.25/(2/3)=1.875 km h-1, avg speed=3.75/(2/3)=5.625 km h-1.

Answer (i) 5 and 5 km h-1 (ii) 0 and 6 km h-1 (iii) 1.875 and 5.625 km h-1.

Which x-t, v-t, a-t graphs cannot represent 1D motion? Is x-t straight for t<0 and parabolic for t>0 meaning particle moves in straight line then parabolic path?

Why?

Velocity and acceleration cannot change abruptly, so graphs with jumps in v or a are impossible. x-t plot shows position over time; a straight segment means constant velocity, parabolic means constant acceleration, not spatial parabola. Suggest context like uniform motion then uniformly accelerated motion.

For x-t plot of oscillatory motion give signs of position, velocity, acceleration at t=0.3 s,1.2 s,-1.2 s; for x-t with three equal intervals which has greatest average speed; for speed-time graph which interval has greatest average acceleration and speed, signs of v and a, accelerations at points A,B,C,D?

Why?

Position sign is x value, velocity sign is slope of x-t, acceleration sign is whether slope increasing (concave up positive) or decreasing. Greatest average speed is steepest |slope| interval, least is flattest. For speed-time, average acceleration magnitude is steepest slope, average speed is average height, at points A-D acceleration is slope there (zero at flat top).

Uniformly accelerated motion

Move a point object with initial velocity and constant acceleration, see x-t, v-t and position update.

Ball dropped from 90 m, loses one tenth of speed at each collision with floor. Plot speed-time graph 0 to 12 s. What happens at each bounce?

Why?

Between bounces speed magnitude grows with slope g. At collision speed reduces to nine-tenths, direction reverses in velocity but speed graph shows drop. Peaks decrease each bounce, total time finite within 12 s plot.

A particle has zero velocity at an instant. What can you say about its acceleration at that instant?

Why?

Zero velocity does not imply zero acceleration. Definitions are exact, kinematic equations only for constant acceleration.

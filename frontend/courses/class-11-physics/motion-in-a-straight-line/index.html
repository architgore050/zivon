Motion in a Straight Line

After this lesson you can:

  • After this lesson you can define velocity, speed and acceleration from graphs and limits.
  • After this lesson you can use kinematic equations for uniform acceleration with correct signs.
  • After this lesson you can solve free fall, stopping distance and relative velocity problems.
  • After this lesson you can distinguish average speed from magnitude of average velocity.

You walk, run and ride a bicycle. Even in sleep air moves and blood flows. Earth rotates once every twenty-four hours.

Motion is change in position with time. Here we study motion along a straight line only.

We treat objects as point objects when size is much smaller than distance moved. Kinematics describes motion without its cause.

In which example can the body be considered approximately a point object? (a) railway carriage moving without jerks (b) monkey on man cycling on circular track (c) spinning cricket ball turning on ground (d) tumbling beaker slipping off table

Why?

A carriage moving without jerks between stations can be treated as point-like; others rotate or tumble so size and shape matter.

Average velocity does not tell speed at one instant. Instantaneous velocity is limit of average velocity as Dt becomes infinitesimal.

It is dx/dt, rate of change of position with time. On x-t graph it is slope of tangent.

For x = 0.08 t^3, as Dt decreases to 0.010 s average velocity approaches 3.84 m s-1 at t = 4.0 s.

Position-time graph and tangent slope

Graph of x = 0.08 t^3 with tangent at chosen time; slope gives instantaneous velocity.

Worked example

Position x = a + b t^2 where a = 8.5 m, b = 2.5 m s-2. Find velocity at t = 0 s and t = 2.0 s, and average velocity between t = 2.0 s and t = 4.0 s.

  1. Differentiate v = dx/dt = 2 b t = 5.0 t m s-1.
  2. At t=0 v = 0 m s-1.
  3. At t=2.0 s v = 5.0 x 2.0 = 10 m s-1.
  4. Average velocity (x(4)-x(2))/(4-2) = 6.0 x 2.5 = 15 m s-1.

Answer v(0)=0 m s-1, v(2.0)=10 m s-1, average velocity 2.0 to 4.0 s = 15 m s-1.

Velocity +24.0 m s-1 and -24.0 m s-1 both have what speed? And how does instantaneous speed compare with magnitude of instantaneous velocity?

Why?

Speed is magnitude of velocity, so both give 24.0 m s-1 and instantaneous speed equals magnitude of instantaneous velocity.

Galileo found rate of change of velocity with time is constant for free fall, while with distance it decreases.

Average acceleration a = (v2-v1)/(t2-t1), SI unit m s-2. On v-t plot it is slope of joining line.

Instantaneous acceleration is dv/dt, slope of tangent to v-t curve. It can be positive, negative or zero.

Deriving kinematic equations

Start with a = dv/dt

dv = a dt, integrate: v - v0 = a t, so v = v0 + a t.

Use v = dx/dt

dx = v dt = (v0 + a t) dt, integrate: x - x0 = v0 t + (1/2) a t^2.

Eliminate t

v dv = a dx, integrate: v^2 - v0^2 = 2 a (x - x0).

Average velocity

For constant acceleration average velocity = (v0+v)/2, so x = average velocity x t.

Worked example

Ball thrown vertically up with 20 m s-1 from top of building 25.0 m from ground. Take g = 10 m s-2. (a) How high will ball rise? (b) How long before it hits ground?

  1. Choose axis Upward positive, v0=+20 m s-1, a=-10 m s-2, v=0 at top.
  2. Height 0 = 20^2 + 2(-10)(y-y0) gives y-y0 = 20 m.
  3. Time up 0 = 20 -10 t1 gives t1=2 s.
  4. Time down From 45 m with v0=0: 0=45+(1/2)(-10)t2^2 gives t2=3 s.
  5. Total t1+t2=5 s. Direct quadratic also gives t=5 s.

Answer (a) 20 m above launch (b) 5 s.

Worked example

Discuss free fall neglecting air resistance. Object released from rest at y=0, upward positive, g=9.8 m s-2.

  1. Acceleration a = -g = -9.8 m s-2 constant.
  2. Velocity v = 0 - g t = -9.8 t m s-1.
  3. Position y = 0 - (1/2) g t^2 = -4.9 t^2 m.
  4. Velocity vs distance v^2 = 0 -2 g y = -19.6 y m2 s-2.

Answer v=-9.8t, y=-4.9t^2, v^2=-19.6y in given units.

Worked example

Prove Galileo's law of odd numbers: distances in successive equal time intervals from rest are in ratio 1:3:5:7...

  1. Use free fall y = -(1/2) g t^2 with t = 0, tau, 2tau, 3tau...
  2. Positions In units of y0 = -(1/2) g tau^2 positions are 0,1,4,9,16...
  3. Differences Successive differences are 1,3,5,7,9,11...
  4. Ratio Distances stand as odd numbers beginning with unity.

Answer Ratio 1:3:5:7:9:11... proved.

Worked example

Derive stopping distance in terms of v0 and deceleration -a. Braking distances 10 m,20 m,34 m,50 m for 11,15,20,25 m/s are given.

  1. Use v^2=v0^2+2ax Final v=0, distance ds.
  2. Solve 0 = v0^2 +2(-a)ds gives ds = -v0^2/(2a) in magnitude v0^2/(2a).
  3. Proportionality ds proportional to v0^2; doubling v0 increases ds by factor 4.

Answer ds = v0^2/(2a) in magnitude, proportional to square of initial velocity.

Worked example

Ruler dropped vertically, caught after falling d=21.0 cm. Estimate reaction time. Take g=9.8 m s-2.

  1. Free fall from rest v0=0, d = (1/2) g t_r^2.
  2. Solve t_r = sqrt(2d/g) = sqrt(2x0.210/9.8) approx 0.21 s.

Answer Reaction time approx 0.21 s.

Choose origin and positive direction first. Signs depend on that choice.

If speeding up acceleration is along velocity; if slowing down it is opposite velocity.

Sign of acceleration alone does not tell if speed increases. Falling with upward positive has negative acceleration but speed increases.

Zero velocity does not mean zero acceleration, as at top of throw.

Car moving along a straight highway with speed of 126 km h-1 is brought to a stop within a distance of 200 m. What is the retardation of the car in m s-2, assumed uniform?

Why?

Use v^2 = v0^2 + 2 a x with v=0 and x=200 m after converting 126 km h-1 to m s-1; magnitude of a is about 3.06 m s-2, and stopping time from v=v0+at is about 11.4 s.

Ball thrown up at 29.4 m s-1, g=9.8 m s-2. What is height reached and return time? Direction of acceleration during upward motion?

Why?

Height = v0^2/(2g)=44.1 m, time up=3 s so return 6 s, acceleration is -g downward throughout.

True or false: (a) zero speed may have non-zero acceleration (b) zero speed may have non-zero velocity (c) constant speed must have zero acceleration (d) positive acceleration must be speeding up

Why?

Zero speed means zero velocity magnitude but acceleration can be non-zero; speed zero cannot have non-zero velocity; constant speed can have acceleration from direction change; positive acceleration sign depends on axis choice.

Average speed is total path length divided by time interval. Average velocity is displacement divided by time interval.

Total path length is greater than or equal to magnitude of displacement, so average speed is greater than or equal to magnitude of average velocity. They are equal when motion is in one direction without turning back.

Example: a man walks 2.5 km at 5 km h-1 to a market and walks back at 7.5 km h-1. In 0 to 50 min he returns home, so displacement is zero and average velocity is zero, but total path is 5 km, so average speed is not zero.

Motion is relative. To understand it we introduce relative velocity: velocity of one object as seen from another moving object.

Example from the exercises: a police van moving at 30 km h-1 fires a bullet at a thief's car speeding away in the same direction at 192 km h-1. Muzzle speed of the bullet is 150 m s-1.

The speed that matters for hitting the thief's car is the bullet speed relative to the thief's car, found from the velocities in the same direction.

On x-t graphs for children A and B from school O to homes P and Q, which statements can be read from slope and intercepts? (a) who lives closer (b) who starts earlier (c) who walks faster (d) same or different arrival (e) overtaking

Why?

x-t graph: final x is distance from school, intercept is start time, slope is velocity, crossing is overtaking.

Why is average speed greater than or equal to magnitude of average velocity, and when is equality true?

Why?

Total path length is at least straight displacement; if particle never reverses direction they are equal.

Why is no distinction needed between instantaneous speed and magnitude of instantaneous velocity?

Why?

Instantaneous speed is defined as magnitude of instantaneous velocity, unlike averages over finite interval.

Woman walks 2.5 km at 5 km h-1 from 9.00 am, stays till 5.00 pm, returns at 25 km h-1. What does her x-t graph look like?

Why?

Slope is velocity: walking slope, zero slope while staying, steep negative slope returning by auto.

A drunkard walking in a narrow lane takes 5 steps forward and 3 steps backward, followed again by 5 steps forward and 3 steps backward, and so on. Each step is 1 m long and requires 1 s. How long in seconds does the drunkard take to fall in a pit 13 m away from the start?

Why?

Plot x-t graph stepwise: each 8 s cycle nets 2 m, reaching 8 m at 32 s, then forward steps reach 13 m at 37 s.

Ball dropped from 90 m, loses one tenth of speed at each floor collision. What does speed-time graph 0 to 12 s show?

Why?

Between bounces speed grows with slope g, at bounce it reverses and magnitude becomes 0.9 of impact speed, so peaks decrease.

Which graphs cannot represent one-dimensional motion, and is x-t plot straight for t<0 and parabola for t>0 a straight-line then parabolic path motion?

Why?

x-t graph is not trajectory; slope is velocity. Discontinuities or infinite slope would imply impossible velocity.

For an x-t plot of one-dimensional motion, how do you read the signs of position, velocity and acceleration at a given time?

Why?

On an x-t graph, x gives position, slope gives velocity, and curvature gives acceleration sign: the graph curves upward for positive acceleration and downward for negative acceleration. On x-t plot with three equal time intervals, how to find interval with greatest average speed and sign of average velocity?

Why?

Average velocity = delta x / delta t, average speed = |delta x|/delta t for monotonic interval; larger magnitude slope means larger speed.

On speed-time graph with three equal intervals, which interval has greatest average acceleration magnitude and greatest average speed? Signs of v and a with positive direction as motion direction?

Why?

Acceleration is slope of v-t graph, speed is value of v; at points A,B,C,D acceleration is slope at that point.

Suggest physical situations for each graph type: (i) x-t straight line (ii) x-t parabola (iii) v-t horizontal (iv) v-t inclined straight line

Why?

Straight x-t = constant velocity, parabola x-t = constant acceleration, horizontal v-t = constant velocity, inclined v-t = constant acceleration.

Man walks 2.5 km at 5 km h-1 to market, turns back at 7.5 km h-1. For interval 0 to 50 min, what are magnitude of average velocity and average speed?

Why?

In 50 min he returns home: displacement zero so average velocity zero, but total path 5 km in 5/6 h gives average speed 6 km h-1.

A police van moving on a highway with a speed of 30 km h-1 fires a bullet at a thief's car speeding away in the same direction with a speed of 192 km h-1. If the muzzle speed of the bullet is 150 m s-1, with what speed in m s-1 does the bullet hit the thief's car relative to it?

Why?

Bullet ground speed is muzzle speed plus van speed, thief speed is converted to m s-1, relative speed is difference of the two ground speeds, about 105 m s-1.

Glossary

Velocity
Rate of change of position with time, dx/dt, with direction.
Acceleration
Rate of change of velocity with time, dv/dt.
Displacement
Change in position from start to end, with sign.
Average speed
Total path length divided by time interval.
Free fall
Fall under gravity neglecting air resistance with uniform acceleration g.

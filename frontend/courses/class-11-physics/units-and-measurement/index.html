Units and Measurement

After this lesson you can:

  • After this lesson you can explain what a unit, a base quantity and a derived unit are.
  • After this lesson you can use significant figures, rounding and uncertainty rules in calculations.
  • After this lesson you can write dimensional formulae and use dimensional analysis to check equations and deduce relations.

You measure length with a scale and mass with a balance. Measurement means comparison with a standard. That standard is called a unit.

The result of measurement is a number with a unit. For example, a length is written as a number in metres.

Units for base quantities are base units. Units for other quantities are derived units. All of them together form a system of units.

What is the result of a measurement?

Why?

Measurement compares a quantity with a unit, so the result is a number with a unit.

Earlier systems were CGS, FPS and MKS. CGS used centimetre, gram and second. FPS used foot, pound and second. MKS used metre, kilogram and second.

SI is now internationally accepted. It was developed by BIPM in 1971 and revised in November 2018. This book follows SI units.

SI has seven base units. Plane angle has unit radian, rad. Solid angle has unit steradian, sr. Both are dimensionless. SI uses the decimal system.

Which set gives SI base units for length, mass and time?

Why?

MKS and SI use metre, kilogram and second for length, mass and time.

Significant figures are reliable digits plus the first uncertain digit. In 1.62 s, 1 and 6 are certain and 2 is uncertain, so there are three significant figures. In 287.5 cm there are four.

All non-zero digits are significant. Zeros between two non-zero digits are significant. Zeros before the first non-zero digit in a number less than 1 are not significant.

Trailing zeros without a decimal point are not significant. Trailing zeros with a decimal point are significant. Changing units does not change significant figures. Precision depends on the least count of the instrument.

Scientific notation and order of magnitude

Write a × 10b

Write the number as a × 10b where a is between 1 and 10 and b is any positive or negative exponent.

Find order of magnitude

Round a to 1 for a ≤ 5 and to 10 for 5 < a ≤ 10. The number is then about 10b and b is the order of magnitude.

Use examples

Earth diameter 1.28 × 107 m is of order 107 m with order 7. Hydrogen atom diameter 1.06 × 10-10 m is of order 10-10 m with order -10.

Trailing zeros

In scientific notation all zeros in a are significant, so 4.700 m = 4.700 × 102 cm has four significant figures.

How many significant figures are in 0.2370 g cm-3?

Why?

Leading zeros are not significant and the trailing zero with a decimal point is significant, so 2, 3, 7 and 0 give four. Similarly 0.007 m2 has 1, 2.64 × 1024 kg has 3, 6.320 J has 4, 6.032 N m-2 has 4 and 0.0006032 m2 has 4.

In multiplication or division, keep as many significant figures as the number with the least significant figures. Density 4.237 g divided by 2.51 cm3 is reported as 1.69 g cm-3, not to many decimal places.

In addition or subtraction, keep as many decimal places as the number with the least decimal places. The sum 436.32 g, 227.2 g and 0.301 g is 663.8 g.

The difference 0.307 m - 0.304 m is 0.003 m. Do not use the multiplication rule for addition and subtraction.

Worked example

5.74 g of a substance occupies 1.2 cm3. Express its density by keeping the significant figures in view.

  1. Count significant figures Mass 5.74 g has 3 significant figures. Volume 1.2 cm3 has 2 significant figures.
  2. Divide Density = 5.74 / 1.2 = 4.783... g cm-3.
  3. Round to least Round to 2 significant figures, the least in the data.

Answer 4.8 g cm-3

Worked example

Each side of a cube is measured to be 7.203 m. What are the total surface area and the volume of the cube to appropriate significant figures?

  1. Count significant figures Length 7.203 m has 4 significant figures, so area and volume need 4.
  2. Surface area Area = 6 × (7.203)2 = 311.299254 m2, rounded to 311.3 m2.
  3. Volume Volume = (7.203)3 = 373.714754 m3, rounded to 373.7 m3.

Answer Surface area 311.3 m2, volume 373.7 m3

Rounding off uncertain digits

Dropped digit more than 5

Raise the preceding digit by 1. Example: 2.746 to three significant figures is 2.75.

Dropped digit less than 5

Leave the preceding digit unchanged. Example: 1.743 to three significant figures is 1.74.

Dropped digit exactly 5

If preceding digit is even, drop the 5. If odd, raise it by 1. So 2.745 becomes 2.74 and 2.735 becomes 2.74.

Intermediate steps

Keep one extra digit in intermediate steps and round at the end to avoid building up rounding errors.

436.32 g + 227.2 g + 0.301 g = ? g to correct decimal places

Why?

Arithmetic sum is 663.821 g, but 227.2 g is correct to only one decimal place, so round to 663.8 g.

Uncertainty in a product combines. For a sheet 16.2 cm by 10.1 cm, each measurement is ± 0.1 cm, so area 163.62 cm2 has about 1.6% error and is quoted as 164 ± 3 cm2.

Subtraction can reduce significant figures. 12.9 g - 7.06 g is 5.8 g, not 5.84 g, because decimal places matter.

Relative error depends on the number itself. Keep one extra digit in intermediate steps of multi-step work.

Both 1.02 g and 9.89 g are accurate to ± 0.01 g. Which has smaller relative error?

Why?

Relative error in 1.02 g is (±0.01/1.02) × 100% = ±1%. In 9.89 g it is (±0.01/9.89) × 100% = ±0.1%, so 9.89 g has smaller relative error.

Dimensions describe the nature of a quantity. Length is [L], mass is [M], time is [T], electric current is [A], temperature is [K], luminous intensity is [cd] and amount of substance is [mol].

Volume is three lengths, so dimensions are [L3]. It has zero dimension in mass and time.

Force is mass times acceleration, so dimensions are [M L T-2]. Magnitudes are not considered, so speed and velocity both have [L T-1].

A dimensional formula shows how base quantities represent a quantity. Volume is [M0 L3 T0]. Speed is [M0 L T-1]. Acceleration is [M0 L T-2]. Mass density is [M L-3 T0].

A dimensional equation equates a quantity with its formula. [V] = [M0 L3 T0], [v] = [M0 L T-1], [F] = [M L T-2] and [ρ] = [M L-3 T0].

You get dimensional equations from relations between quantities by replacing each quantity with its dimensions.

Checking x = x0 + v0 t + (1/2) a t2

Left side

[x] = [L].

First term right

[x0] = [L].

Second term right

[v0 t] = [L T-1][T] = [L].

Third term right

[(1/2) a t2] = [L T-2][T2] = [L].

Conclusion

Each term has dimension of length, same as left side, so the equation is dimensionally correct. Passing does not prove it right.

Worked example

Let us consider an equation 1/2 m v2 = m g h where m is the mass of the body, v its velocity, g is the acceleration due to gravity and h is the height. Check whether this equation is dimensionally correct.

  1. Left side [M][L T-1]2 = [M][L2 T-2] = [M L2 T-2].
  2. Right side [M][L T-2][L] = [M][L2 T-2] = [M L2 T-2].
  3. Compare Dimensions of LHS and RHS are the same, so the equation is dimensionally correct.

Answer Dimensionally correct

Worked example

The SI unit of energy is J = kg m2 s-2; that of speed v is m s-1 and of acceleration a is m s-2. Which of the formulae for kinetic energy (K) can you rule out on dimensional arguments (m stands for mass): (a) K = m2 v3 (b) K = (1/2) m v2 (c) K = m a (d) K = (3/16) m v2 (e) K = (1/2) m v2 + m a

  1. Dimensions of K K has dimensions [M L2 T-2] from J = kg m2 s-2.
  2. Check each formula (a) gives [M2 L3 T-3], (b) and (d) give [M L2 T-2], (c) gives [M L T-2], (e) adds quantities of different dimensions so has no proper dimensions.
  3. Rule out (a), (c) and (e) are ruled out. Dimensions cannot distinguish (b) and (d). The correct formula is (b).

Answer (a), (c) and (e) ruled out; correct is (b) K = (1/2) m v2

Deducing pendulum period by dimensions

Assume product form

Suppose period T depends on length l, mass m and gravity g as T = k lx gy mz, where k is dimensionless and x, y, z are exponents.

Match dimensions

[T] = [L]x [L T-2]y [M]z gives Lx+y T-2y Mz, so x + y = 0, -2y = 1 and z = 0.

Solve

x = 1/2, y = -1/2, z = 0, so T = k l1/2 g-1/2 = k sqrt(l/g).

Constant

k cannot be obtained by dimensions. Actually k = 2π, so T = 2π sqrt(l/g).

Pendulum period versus length

Shows period T = 2π sqrt(l/g) for a simple pendulum as length changes.

Which addition is allowed by the principle of homogeneity?

Why?

Only quantities with the same dimensions can be added or subtracted. Length plus length has the same dimension [L] on both sides.

Base quantities are length, mass, time, electric current, temperature, amount of substance and luminous intensity. Their units are metre, kilogram, second, ampere, kelvin, mole and candela.

Derived units can have special names such as joule, newton and watt. Units in calculations are treated as algebraic quantities.

Only proper significant figures should be retained. Dimensions check equations, but a dimensionally correct equation need not be exact and a dimensionally wrong equation must be wrong.

Worked example

Fill in the blanks: (a) volume of a cube of side 1 cm in m3 (b) vehicle moving with speed 18 km h-1 covers m in 1 s (c) 1 kg m2 s-2 = g cm2 s-2 (d) 3.0 m s-2 = km h-2 (e) 1 m = ly (f) G = 6.67 × 10-11 N m2 (kg)-2 = (cm)3 s-2 g-1

  1. Cube and vehicle 1 cm = 0.01 m, so volume = (0.01)3 = 1 × 10-6 m3. 18 km h-1 = 5 m s-1, so in 1 s it covers 5 m.
  2. Energy unit 1 kg = 1000 g and 1 m = 100 cm, so 1 kg m2 s-2 = 1000 × 10000 = 1 × 107 g cm2 s-2.
  3. Acceleration 3.0 m s-2 = 0.003 km per (1/3600 h)2 = 38880 km h-2.
  4. Light year and G Light year is 9.47 × 1015 m from the source example, so 1 m = 1.06 × 10-16 ly. For G, 1 N = 105 g cm s-2, giving 6.67 × 10-8 (cm)3 s-2 g-1.

Answer 1 × 10-6 m3; 5 m; 1 × 107 g cm2 s-2; 38880 km h-2; 1.06 × 10-16 ly; 6.67 × 10-8 (cm)3 s-2 g-1

Calorie in new units

Start with calorie

A calorie equals about 4.2 J and 1 J = 1 kg m2 s-2.

New units

New unit of mass equals α kg, length equals β m and time equals γ s. Treat units as algebraic quantities.

Convert

1 kg = 1/α new mass units, 1 m = 1/β new length units, 1 s = 1/γ new time units, so 1 kg m2 s-2 = α-1 β-2 γ2 new units.

Result

Calorie magnitude is 4.2 α-1 β-2 γ2 in the new units.

Which statement is meaningful without specifying a standard for comparison?

Why?

Large or small is meaningless without a standard. Proton compared with electron specifies the standard, the electron, while the others do not specify a standard.

A new unit of length is chosen so speed of light in vacuum is unity. Light takes 8 min and 20 s from Sun to Earth. What is the distance in the new unit?

Why?

Time is 8 × 60 + 20 = 500 s. With speed = 1 in the new unit, distance = speed × time = 500 new units.

Which is the most precise device for measuring length: (a) vernier callipers with 20 divisions on sliding scale (b) screw gauge of pitch 1 mm and 100 divisions on circular scale (c) optical instrument that can measure length to within a wavelength of light?

Why?

Precision depends on least count. The optical instrument measures to within a wavelength of light, the smallest scale in the list, so it is the most precise.

Worked example

A student measures the thickness of a human hair by looking at it through a microscope of magnification 100. He makes 20 observations and finds that the average width of the hair in the field of view of the microscope is 3.5 mm. What is the estimate on the thickness of hair?

  1. Use magnification Actual thickness = observed width / magnification = 3.5 mm / 100.
  2. Calculate 3.5 / 100 = 0.035 mm.
  3. Significant figures 3.5 mm has 2 significant figures, so answer is 0.035 mm = 3.5 × 10-5 m.

Answer 0.035 mm or 3.5 × 10-5 m

Why is a set of 100 measurements of the diameter of a thin brass rod expected to yield a more reliable estimate than a set of 5 measurements only?

Why?

More observations improve the average estimate. The same principle applies to estimating thread diameter with a metre scale and to repeated measurements in general.

Worked example

The photograph of a house occupies an area of 1.75 cm2 on a 35 mm slide. The slide is projected on to a screen, and the area of the house on the screen is 1.55 m2. What is the linear magnification of the projector-screen arrangement?

  1. Convert slide area 1.75 cm2 = 1.75 × 10-4 m2.
  2. Area ratio Area ratio = 1.55 / (1.75 × 10-4) = 8857.
  3. Linear magnification Linear magnification = sqrt(area ratio) = sqrt(8857) = 94.1 to three significant figures.

Answer 94.1

The mass of a box measured by a grocer's balance is 2.30 kg. Two gold pieces of masses 20.15 g and 20.17 g are added to the box. What is (a) the total mass of the box, (b) the difference in the masses of the pieces to correct significant figures?

A boy writes m/m0 = (1 - v2)-1/2 and forgets where to put c. Where must c go for dimensional correctness?

Why?

v and c both have dimensions [L T-1], so v/c is dimensionless. The argument of the function must be dimensionless, and ratio of similar quantities is dimensionless.

Worked example

The unit of length convenient on the atomic scale is known as an angstrom and is denoted by Å: 1 Å = 10-10 m. The size of a hydrogen atom is about 0.5 Å. What is the total atomic volume in m3 of a mole of hydrogen atoms?

  1. Volume per atom Size = 0.5 Å = 0.5 × 10-10 m. Volume per atom = (0.5 × 10-10)3 = 1.25 × 10-31 m3.
  2. Number per mole One mole contains 6.02214076 × 1023 elementary entities from the mole definition.
  3. Total volume Total = 1.25 × 10-31 × 6.022 × 1023 = 7.5 × 10-8 m3.

Answer 7.5 × 10-8 m3

One mole of an ideal gas at standard temperature and pressure occupies 22.4 L. Taking size of hydrogen molecule as about 1 Å, what is the ratio of molar volume to atomic volume of a mole of hydrogen?

Why?

Molar volume = 22.4 L = 2.24 × 10-2 m3. Atomic volume = (1 × 10-10)3 × 6.022 × 1023 = 6.0 × 10-7 m3. Ratio = 2.24 × 10-2 / 6.0 × 10-7 = 3.7 × 104. The ratio is large because in a gas atoms are far apart and most of the volume is empty space between them.

If you look out of a fast moving train, nearby trees seem to move rapidly opposite to the train but distant objects seem stationary. Why?

Why?

Apparent motion depends on how fast the direction to the object changes for the moving observer. Nearby objects change direction quickly, distant objects change direction slowly, so they seem to move with you or be stationary.

Worked example

The Sun is a hot plasma with inner core temperature exceeding 107 K and outer surface about 6000 K. Expect the mass density of the Sun to be in the range of solids and liquids or gases? Check with mass of the Sun = 2.0 × 1030 kg, radius of the Sun = 7.0 × 108 m.

  1. Volume of Sun Volume = (4/3) π r3 = (4/3) π (7.0 × 108)3 = 1.44 × 1027 m3.
  2. Density Density = mass / volume = 2.0 × 1030 / 1.44 × 1027 = 1.4 × 103 kg m-3.
  3. Compare range 1.4 × 103 kg m-3 is in the range of solids and liquids, not gases, so the guess for solids and liquids range is correct.

Answer 1.4 × 103 kg m-3, in the range of solids and liquids

What does dimensional analysis tell us about an equation?

Why?

If an equation fails the test it is proved wrong. If it passes, it is not proved right. Constants cannot be obtained and quantities with same dimensions cannot be distinguished.

Glossary

unit
A standard reference with which a physical quantity is compared.
base quantity
A fundamental quantity chosen for a system of units, such as length, mass and time.
derived unit
A unit formed as a combination of base units for a derived quantity.
significant figures
Reliable digits plus the first uncertain digit in a measurement.
dimension
Powers of base quantities that represent a physical quantity, written in square brackets.
dimensional formula
Expression showing which base quantities represent the dimensions of a quantity.
order of magnitude
The exponent b in a × 10b for an approximate estimate of a number.
principle of homogeneity
Only quantities with the same dimensions can be added or subtracted.

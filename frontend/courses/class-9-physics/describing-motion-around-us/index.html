Everything around us is in motion, from butterflies and horses to clouds and tides. To study this variety simply, scientists first study idealised forms such as linear motion.

Linear motion is motion in a straight line. It is the simplest kind of motion. Examples are a ball falling vertically, a car on a straight highway and a train on a straight track.

To describe position we first fix a reference point. The distance and direction from that point at any instant describe the position.

If position changes with time, the object is in motion. If position does not change with time, the object is at rest. On a straight line, right of O is positive and left of O is negative.

An object is said to be in motion when its position with respect to the reference point changes with time. What does this mean for a truck ahead of you?

Why?

Motion is judged by change of position with respect to a reference point, not by speed alone. Distance and direction together describe position.

An athlete starts at O, reaches B, then A, then runs back to B. Total distance travelled is OA + AB = 100 m + 60 m = 160 m.

Displacement between start and stop is OB = 40 m in the positive direction. Total distance and magnitude of displacement are not equal when the object turns back.

Displacement needs direction and magnitude. Total distance needs only a number. Both have SI unit metre.

A ball is thrown vertically upwards from O, moves to B and falls back to O. Which statement about displacement is true?

Why?

When the ball returns to O displacement is zero while total distance is not zero. Magnitude of displacement can never exceed total distance, and it equals total distance only when moving in one direction.

Average speed is total distance travelled divided by the time interval. It tells how fast an object moves and has no direction.

Equal distances in equal intervals means uniform motion at constant speed. Unequal distances mean non-uniform motion with increasing or decreasing speed.

Worked example

Two postmen start walking towards each other from a distance of 210 yojanas. One travels 9 yojanas per day and the other covers 5 yojanas per day. In how many days will they meet?

  1. Distance covered together in one day 9 yojanas + 5 yojanas = 14 yojanas per day.
  2. Time to cover 210 yojanas 210 / 14 = 15 days.
  3. Check distances First postman 9 x 15 = 135 yojanas. Second postman 5 x 15 = 75 yojanas. Total 210 yojanas.

Answer They meet after 15 days.

Average velocity is displacement divided by the time interval. It is written as v_av = s/t.

Its direction is the same as displacement and is shown by plus or minus sign. Its SI unit is m s-1, also measured in km h-1. It is the average rate of change of position with time.

Worked example

Sarang takes 50 seconds to swim from one end to the other end and back in a swimming pool. Find his average speed and average velocity in 50 s.

  1. Total distance Total distance travelled = 50 m.
  2. Displacement He returns to the start, so displacement = 0 m.
  3. Average speed 50 m / 50 s = 1 m s-1.
  4. Average velocity 0 m / 50 s = 0 m s-1.

Answer Average speed 1 m s-1, average velocity 0 m s-1.

You drive 200 km north in three hours, then 200 km south in two hours. What is the average speed for the entire trip?

Why?

Total distance = 400 km. Total time = 5 h. Average speed = 400 / 5 = 80 km h-1. Displacement is zero, so average velocity is zero.

Average acceleration is change in velocity divided by the time interval. It is (v - u) / (t2 - t1) with SI unit m s-2.

If magnitude of velocity increases, acceleration is in the direction of velocity. If magnitude decreases, acceleration is opposite to velocity.

Acceleration can come from change in magnitude, change in direction, or both.

Worked example

A bus moves with velocity 36 km h-1. The driver presses the accelerator for 10 s and velocity increases to 54 km h-1. Then the bus stops in 5 s when brakes are pressed. Find average acceleration in both intervals.

  1. Convert velocities 36 km h-1 = 10 m s-1. 54 km h-1 = 15 m s-1.
  2. Accelerator interval a = (15 - 10) / 10 = 0.5 m s-2 in the direction of velocity.
  3. Brake interval a = (0 - 15) / 5 = -3 m s-2. Minus sign means opposite to velocity.

Answer (i) 0.5 m s-2 in direction of velocity. (ii) -3 m s-2 opposite to velocity.

Worked example

An object dropped from a height has velocities 0 m s-1 at 0 s, 9.8 m s-1 at 1 s, 19.6 m s-1 at 2 s, 29.4 m s-1 at 3 s and 39.2 m s-1 at 4 s. Find average acceleration in each 1 s interval.

  1. 0 s to 1 s (9.8 - 0) / 1 = 9.8 m s-2.
  2. 1 s to 2 s, 2 s to 3 s, 3 s to 4 s Each interval gives (19.6-9.8)/1, (29.4-19.6)/1 and (39.2-29.4)/1 = 9.8 m s-2.
  3. Conclusion Acceleration is constant at 9.8 m s-2 in the direction of motion. This is g, acceleration due to gravity.

Answer Constant average acceleration 9.8 m s-2 in direction of motion.

A scooter speedometer reading is constant. Can the scooter still be accelerating?

Why?

Acceleration occurs when velocity changes in magnitude or direction or both. In circular motion speed is constant but direction changes, so acceleration is non-zero.

Graphs show how position, velocity or acceleration changes with time. They help compare motions and find quantities.

To plot, draw X and Y axes from origin O, choose time on X-axis and position on Y-axis, choose a suitable scale, mark points and join them.

Plotting a position-time graph

Draw axes

Draw perpendicular lines OX and OY meeting at origin O on graph paper.

Choose scale

For Table 4.3 use X-axis 5 divisions = 1 s and Y-axis 5 divisions = 20 m.

Mark points

At 0 s position 0 m is origin. At 1 s position 20 m, at 2 s 40 m, and so on up to 6 s 120 m.

Join points

Joining the points gives a straight line for constant velocity data.

Worked example

For a vehicle starting from rest and speeding up, positions are 0 m at 0 s, 1 m at 2 s, 4 m at 4 s, 9 m at 6 s, 16 m at 8 s, 25 m at 10 s and 36 m at 12 s. Plot the position-time graph.

  1. Choose scale X-axis 5 divisions = 2 s, Y-axis 5 divisions = 5 m.
  2. Mark points Mark each time-position pair from the table.
  3. Join points Points do not fall on a straight line. Join by a curve.

Answer The graph is a curve, showing velocity is increasing.

Position-time graph and velocity

A position-time graph is drawn from the equation position = velocity x time for constant velocity. Changing the velocity changes the steepness of the line.

A straight line position-time graph means constant velocity. A curved graph means velocity is not constant.

A line parallel to the time axis means position is not changing, so the object is at rest. A steeper line means higher velocity.

Worked example

A position-time graph shows a vehicle at 40 m from the origin and position not changing with time. What does it indicate?

  1. Read position Position stays at 40 m for all times.
  2. Interpret line Line is parallel to time axis.

Answer The vehicle is at rest at 40 m from the origin.

Worked example

Position-time graphs of two objects A and B are given. Which object has higher magnitude of average velocity?

  1. Compare displacement in same time Draw lines parallel to axes for the same time interval.
  2. Compare slopes Displacement of B is more than A in the same time, so slope of B is steeper.

Answer Velocity of B is higher than velocity of A.

Velocity from position-time slope

Take part AB of graph

Draw lines parallel to axes from A and B to form triangle ABC.

Identify sides

BC is change in position s2 - s1. CA is change in time t2 - t1.

Divide

Velocity = BC / CA = (s2 - s1)/(t2 - t1). Example (80 - 40)/(4 - 2) = 20 m s-1.

A velocity-time graph shows change in velocity with time. Constant velocity gives a line parallel to X-axis and zero acceleration.

Increasing velocity with a straight line gives constant acceleration in the direction of velocity. Decreasing velocity gives constant acceleration opposite to velocity.

Velocity-time graph slope and area

A velocity-time graph is computed from initial velocity and constant acceleration. Slope gives acceleration and area under the line gives displacement.

Slope and area from velocity-time graph

Slope for acceleration

Acceleration = change in velocity / change in time. Between 10 s and 20 s, (10 - 5)/10 = 0.5 m s-2. For decreasing graph it is -0.5 m s-2.

Area for displacement, constant velocity

Area of rectangle = velocity x time. 20 m s-1 x 6 s = 120 m.

Area for displacement, changing velocity

Between 10 s and 20 s area = rectangle + triangle = 50 m + 25 m = 75 m.

Kinematic equations describe motion with constant acceleration. Acceleration at each instant equals average acceleration.

The five quantities are displacement s, time t, initial velocity u, final velocity v and acceleration a. Choose the equation that fits the known quantities.

Deriving kinematic equations

Velocity equation

From a = (v - u)/t we get at = v - u, so v = u + at.

Displacement equation

Displacement is area under velocity-time graph: rectangle + triangle gives s = ut + 1/2 at^2.

Velocity squared equation

Eliminate t between the two equations to get v^2 = u^2 + 2as.

Worked example

A car brakes with acceleration -4 m s-2. How much distance before stopping if it was moving at (i) 54 km h-1 and (ii) 108 km h-1?

  1. Convert velocities (i) 54 km h-1 = 15 m s-1. (ii) 108 km h-1 = 30 m s-1. Final velocity v = 0.
  2. Use v^2 = u^2 + 2as 0 = u^2 + 2 x (-4) x s, so s = u^2 / 8.
  3. Calculate (i) 225/8 = 28.1 m. (ii) 900/8 = 112.5 m.

Answer (i) 28.1 m (ii) 112.5 m. Higher initial speed needs much larger stopping distance.

Which pair are the remaining two kinematic equations derived from v = u + at and s = ut + 1/2 at^2?

Why?

The second equation comes from area of a trapezium under the velocity-time graph, giving s = (u + v)/2 t. The first follows by substitution.

Motion in a plane is motion in two dimensions. Examples are a kicked ball, a satellite and a vehicle overtaking another.

Uniform circular motion is motion in a circular path with constant speed. One revolution distance is the circumference, displacement is zero, average speed is 2 pi R / T and average velocity is zero.

Uniform circular motion

A point moves on a circle at constant speed. Its velocity arrow is always along the tangent and changes direction continuously.

Velocity at a point is along the tangent to the circle in the direction of motion. A tangent meets the circle at only one point.

When a marble moving inside a ring is released, it moves in a straight line in the direction at the instant of release.

Uniform circular motion is an idealised model. It is useful for planets around the Sun and vehicles making circular turns.

A man goes from home to a shop 250 m away, returns home, goes to the shop again and returns home. What are total distance and displacement from home?

Why?

Four journeys of 250 m give total distance 1000 m. He ends at home, so net change in position is zero.

A car starts from rest and its velocity reaches 24 m s-1 in 6 s. Find average acceleration and distance travelled in 6 s.

Why?

a = (24 - 0)/6 = 4 m s-2. s = ut + 1/2 at^2 = 0 + 0.5 x 4 x 36 = 72 m.

A motorbike moving with initial velocity 28 m s-1 and constant acceleration stops after travelling 98 m. Find acceleration and time to stop.

Why?

0 = 28^2 + 2 x a x 98 gives a = -4 m s-2. Time = (0 - 28)/(-4) = 7 s.

A truck at 54 km h-1 slows to 36 km h-1 in 36 s with constant acceleration. What distance does it travel?

Why?

Convert to 15 m s-1 and 10 m s-1. Average velocity = 12.5 m s-1. Distance = 12.5 x 36 = 450 m.

A car accelerates uniformly from rest to 20 m s-1 in 5 s, travels at 20 m s-1 for 10 s, then brakes uniformly to stop in 6 s. Find total distance.

Why?

Phase 1: a = 4 m s-2, s = 50 m. Phase 2: s = 200 m. Phase 3: a = -3.33 m s-2, s = 60 m. Total = 310 m.

On a velocity-time graph, what does the area between the graph and the time axis represent?

Why?

Area = velocity x time for constant velocity and area of the shape for changing velocity. Both give displacement in that time interval.

The Earth moves around the Sun. Can an object kept on the Earth be considered at rest?

Why?

Rest and motion depend on the reference point. With respect to Earth the object is at rest, even though Earth moves around the Sun.

Two objects have the same initial and final positions over 10 s on position-time graphs. What can you say about their average velocity over 10 s?

Why?

Average velocity = displacement / time. Same initial and final positions give same displacement, and time is the same, so average velocities are equal. Average speed depends on total distance, which may differ.

Glossary

displacement
Net change in position between two instants, with magnitude and direction.
average velocity
Displacement divided by the time interval.
average acceleration
Change in velocity divided by the time interval.
slope
Steepness of a graph line, giving a rate of change.
uniform circular motion
Motion in a circle at constant speed with changing velocity direction.
tangent
A line touching a circle at only one point.

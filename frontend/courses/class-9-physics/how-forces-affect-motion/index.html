How Forces Affect Motion

You push a box, kick a ball, or squeeze a lemon. In each case, something changes. A force can make an object move from rest. It can change the speed or direction of a moving object. It can even change the shape of an object.

A force is not just a number. You must say how strong it is and which way it acts. The SI unit of force is the newton. Its symbol is N.

In real life, more than one force often acts on an object. When you push a box, friction acts between the box and the floor in the opposite direction. The box starts moving only when your applied force is larger than the friction force.

If two teams pull a rope with equal forces in opposite directions, the forces are balanced. The rope does not move. If one team pulls harder, the forces are unbalanced and the rope moves in the direction of the larger force.

For forces in the same direction, add their magnitudes. For opposite unequal forces, subtract the smaller from the larger. The net force points along the larger force.

A block has a 10 N force acting to the right and a 6 N force acting to the left. What is the net force?

Why?

Opposite unequal forces subtract. 10 N - 6 N = 4 N, and the direction is along the larger 10 N force.

If you stop pushing a moving box, friction keeps acting against its motion. The box slows down and comes to rest. On a surface with smaller friction, the object travels farther before stopping.

When you walk, your foot pushes the ground backwards. The ground applies a forward force of friction on your foot. This forward friction helps you move.

Net force on a block

Adjust two horizontal forces on a block and see how the net force changes.

If the force of friction is zero, a moving object does not need a further force to keep moving with constant velocity. It keeps moving because no net force changes its velocity. This is Newton's first law.

Newton's first law says an object at rest remains at rest, and an object in motion continues with constant velocity, unless a net force acts upon it. Constant velocity means no change in the magnitude or direction of velocity.

If the net force is zero, the object cannot start moving or change velocity. Its acceleration is zero. A moving object with zero net force keeps its velocity unchanged.

An object is moving with constant velocity. What can you say about the net force acting on it?

Why?

Newton's first law says an object continues with constant velocity unless a net force acts. Constant velocity means no net force.

A net force produces acceleration. A gentle push gives a small acceleration. A strong push gives a larger acceleration.

For the same force, a lighter object accelerates more than a heavier object. The acceleration is proportional to the net force and inversely proportional to the mass. This idea is Newton's second law.

Newton's second law is written as F = ma. Here F is net force, m is mass, and a is acceleration. The acceleration has the same direction as the net force.

One newton is the force that produces an acceleration of 1 m s–2 on an object of mass 1 kg. The gravitational force on mass m is F = mg, where g = 9.8 m s–2. For quick estimations, g can be taken as 10 m s–2.

Finding acceleration from force

Find the net force

Add forces acting in the same direction. Subtract opposite unequal forces and take the direction of the larger force.

Use Newton's second law

Use a = F/m. The acceleration is in the direction of the net force.

If the force is weight

Use F = mg, where g = 9.8 m s–2.

Worked example

A stationary block of 25 kg is on a horizontal floor. The maximum force of friction opposing motion is 50 N. If a student pushes it with a constant force of 55 N in the forward direction, what is the displacement in 2 seconds?

  1. Find the net force The applied force is 55 N and the maximum friction is 50 N. The net force is 55 N - 50 N = 5 N.
  2. Find the acceleration Using F = ma, the acceleration is 5 N / 25 kg = 0.2 m s–2.
  3. Find the displacement The displacement in 2 seconds is 0.4 m.

Answer 0.4 m in the forward direction.

A force needs at least two objects to interact. When you push a table, the table pushes you back in the opposite direction. This pair of forces acts on different objects.

Newton's third law says whenever one object exerts a force on a second object, the second object simultaneously exerts an equal and opposite force on the first object. These forces do not cancel each other because they act on different objects. Equal forces do not always give equal accelerations, because the masses may be different.

A bullet and a gun exert equal forces on each other. Why does the bullet have a much larger acceleration?

Why?

The forces are equal in magnitude, but acceleration depends on mass. The bullet has much smaller mass, so its acceleration is larger.

Which statement correctly describes Newton's second law?

Why?

Newton's second law says acceleration is in the direction of the net force, proportional to net force, and inversely proportional to mass.

Glossary

force
A push or pull that can change an object's motion or shape. It has magnitude and direction.
net force
The single force that results from all forces acting on an object.
balanced forces
Forces equal in magnitude and opposite in direction that give zero net force.
friction
A force between surfaces in contact. It can oppose an applied force or a slipping tendency.
inertia
The tendency of objects to resist change in their state of rest or uniform motion.
constant velocity
Velocity whose magnitude and direction do not change.
